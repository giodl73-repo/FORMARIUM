# Bounded-Question Composition and Evaluation Guide

Status: internal F100 candidate Book One route; not `preview-01`, publication,
or external-reader evidence

## The question this guide answers

Use this route when finding one entry is not enough: the question depends on
several concepts whose constraints, evidence, interactions, exclusions, and
operating conditions must be considered together.

The route produces a bounded working closure and an inspectable evaluation.
It does not discover truth automatically, replace domain judgment, or prove
that the selected concepts are sufficient.

## Great default

Start with six visible fields:

| Field | Reader question |
|---|---|
| Question | What exact decision, explanation, comparison, or check is needed? |
| Working concepts | Which canonical senses or views are required now? |
| Decisive constraints | What can invalidate or exclude an otherwise attractive result? |
| Result state | Is the route complete, incomplete, contradictory, or truncated? |
| Unresolved frontier | What required concept, evidence, condition, or authority is still missing? |
| Next action | Inspect, obtain evidence, revise context, reconcile, defer, or decide under named authority? |

Book mode adds short explanations and nearby contrasts. Full mode exposes the
typed graph, relation evidence, factor overlap, checks, projection loss,
version identities, and limitations. Compact mode may hide detail, but never
the result state, decisive constraint, unresolved frontier, or declared loss.

## F99 integrated route

1. Declare the exact question, subject, population, context, time, authority,
   and intended disposition.
2. Select canonical senses before expanding a word label; record rejected
   senses when ambiguity could change the route.
3. Seed required concepts, then follow only reviewed typed relations under an
   explicit direction, follow policy, and work budget.
4. Add manual nodes only with a reason and class: required, evaluative,
   explanatory, provenance, optional, excluded, or unresolved.
5. Apply union, join, exclusion, and interaction as typed graph operations.
   Preserve identity, overlap, cardinality, contradictions, and conditional
   effects instead of borrowing arithmetic semantics.
6. Run constraint, diagnostic, procedure, formula-scope, and completeness
   checks. A pass is local to its exact criterion and evidence.
7. Evaluate components before aggregating. Preserve units/scales, directions,
   comparators, uncertainty, dependencies, interactions, noncompensation,
   missingness, authority, and sensitivity.
8. Project a readable answer with selected, rejected, and unresolved rows.
   State every omission, merge, deduplication, generalization, and inverse
   limit as declared loss.
9. Reconcile the selected result, excluded alternatives, conflicts, frontier,
   residuals, and next action. Keep explanation, recommendation, authorized
   decision, action, and outcome separate.
10. Freeze the reference, relation, query, context, evidence, evaluation,
    projection, and guide versions needed for another person to replay the
    route.

## What an answer may say

An answer may report that a selected route passes named checks under declared
conditions, that one alternative is excluded by an exact constraint, that
the result is sensitive to a factor or interaction, or that the closure is
incomplete, contradictory, or truncated.

It may not turn internal simulation into reader evidence, a scenario into a
forecast, correlation into causation, commitment into delivery, absence of an
incident into safety, a score into universal value, or a projected summary
into its full source.
