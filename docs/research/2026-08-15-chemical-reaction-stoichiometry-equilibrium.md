# Chemical Reaction, Stoichiometry, Rate, and Equilibrium Research Note

Date: 2026-08-15
Status: source-grounded candidate synthesis

## Question

Which reusable concepts explain chemical change without turning the reference
into a list of named reactions or mistaking equations for mechanisms?

## Finding

Chemical change needs four linked but distinct views:

```text
process: species interconvert
accounting: stoichiometric numbers relate amount changes through reaction extent
kinetics: rates and rate laws describe change through time
thermodynamics: activities and equilibrium characterize directional balance
```

A reaction equation is a symbolic representation, not evidence that the
reaction occurs in one elementary step. A proposed mechanism must also fit
stoichiometry, kinetics, intermediates, and other experimental evidence.

Reactants are consumed and products formed under the selected reaction
description. Stoichiometric numbers are negative for reactants and positive for
products. Extent of reaction supplies one progress coordinate for a declared
reaction equation. Rate of reaction is distinct from an individual species'
appearance or disappearance rate and requires extra care for stepwise reactions,
intermediate accumulation, side products, or changing volume.

Chemical equilibrium is dynamic: forward and reverse rates are equal under the
declared conditions. The equilibrium constant depends on a specified activity
or other quantity convention and reaction equation. A catalyst changes rate or
pathway but not the overall standard Gibbs-energy change or equilibrium constant.

## Concept-taxonomy boundary

Named reaction families, named catalysts, and named mechanisms remain examples
or specialist-domain content. The canonical entry owns process roles,
stoichiometric accounting, progress, time rate, equilibrium, and evidence for a
mechanism—not a catalog of reactions.
