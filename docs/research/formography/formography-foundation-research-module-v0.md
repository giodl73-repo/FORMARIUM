# Formography Foundation Research Module v0

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: `proceed_with_targeted_narrowing`
Evidence class: internal method audit plus source-reviewed external comparison

## Research question

Can **Formography** name a distinct, useful, and falsifiable field for mapping
forms through factors and relations while preserving context, boundaries,
provenance, alternative decompositions, and declared loss?

## Working definition

> Formography is the systematic, source-custodied mapping of context-bounded
> forms through alternative factorization, typed relations, and loss-declared
> projections without treating any one map as a universal ontology.

The definition is provisional. `Form` does not mean visual shape alone. In the
working claim, a form is an identifiable organization of factors and relations
under a declared question, perspective, context, scale, and evidence state.
`Source-custodied` means that claims, mappings, versions, losses, and review
state remain reconstructable.

## Smallest falsifiable thesis

```text
working Factorium reference method
+ missing field-level account of factor-and-relation mapping
-> a source-bounded Formography method that can be compared, criticized,
   taught, and reproduced without replacing the reference's editorial owners
```

The thesis fails if established neighboring fields already own the complete
method, if the proposed vocabulary cannot distinguish method from artifact, or
if independent reviewers cannot apply the method consistently to bounded
cases.

## Existing owner system

Factorium remains authoritative for its current artifacts:

- canonical entry and sense identity;
- Factor Tables and specialized reference views;
- typed relation kinds and qualifiers;
- source custody, maturity, and role review;
- Reader routes and Factor Guides;
- publication and rendering policy.

Formography is not a new authority over those artifacts. It is a candidate
field-level description of the mapping method already partially exercised by
them.

## Current workaround and cost

The repository currently explains its work through several overlapping
phrases: decomposition patterns, factorization, typed graph maintenance,
mapping views, progressive abstraction, and bounded composition. These are
individually accurate but do not provide one externally comparable name for
the whole mapping practice.

The cost is conceptual fragmentation:

- method claims are distributed across specifications;
- neighboring fields cannot be compared against one stable contract;
- research papers risk describing the publication before defining the method;
- `Factorium`, `Tables`, graph, factoring, and atlas metaphors can be confused.

## Local source audit

### Established local behavior

1. `specs/FACTOR-TABLE-ENTRY.md` separates senses from competing
   factorizations and requires pivots, supporting roles, constraints,
   interactions, mechanism mappings, cross-references, maturity, and
   provenance.
2. `specs/FACTORIUM-ENTRY-GRAPH.md` maintains entries, senses, and typed views
   as a graph with canonical relationships, contextualization, alternatives,
   contrasts, and progressive disclosure.
3. `specs/FACTORIUM-REFERENCE-SIDECARS.md` requires typed directed relations,
   kind-specific qualifiers, exact source paths, and digest-bound review.
4. `PRODUCT_PLAN.md` rejects one universal decomposition, separates general
   factor roles from mechanisms, and keeps software auxiliary.
5. The Mapping Integrity Auditor requires source, target, direction, version,
   domain, cardinality, loss, exceptions, and authority.

### What local evidence does not establish

- that `Formography` is lexically unoccupied;
- that the complete method is novel;
- that the method is a coherent academic field;
- that readers or researchers will adopt the vocabulary;
- that Factorium's current corpus is sufficient empirical validation;
- that `form`, `factor`, `relation`, or `map` have domain-independent
  definitions.

## External prior-art result

### Exact term collision

A 2021 TU Dresden workshop call by Ingmar Lippert, Ann-Kristin Kühnen, and
Susann Wagenknecht proposed the exact term:

> "We propose formography---the detailed study of forms in (and of) practice,
> an approach that draws upon ethnographic observation and works with analytic
> perspectives from fields such as sociology, social theory, anthropology,
> and STS."

Source:
https://tu-dresden.de/gsw/phil/iso/technosozial/forschung/forms

The confirmed prior use studies how forms are fabricated, performed,
appropriated, and contested in social practice through ethnographic inquiry.
No peer-reviewed publication using `formography` as its named field was
confirmed in the reviewed sources. Publication status remains
`UNCONFIRMED`, not absent.

The Factorium proposal must acknowledge this cognate use. Its candidate
distinction is a cross-domain, inspectable mapping method based on alternative
factorization, typed and scoped relations, source custody, and loss-declared
projections rather than an ethnographic study of practice-forms.

### Strongest methodological precedents

| Precedent | What it already owns | Required Formography distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Novak and Cañas concept maps | explicit relations between concepts and hierarchical arrangement | no required hierarchy; factors, evidence, boundaries, alternatives, and projection loss |
| Ranganathan faceted classification | analytico-synthetic subject decomposition and multiple points of view | no fixed PMEST factor set; instance-level relation and evidence custody |
| ISO Topic Maps | topics, associations, occurrences, and scope as assertion context | method for discovering and testing forms rather than an encoding standard |
| Caroline Levine's *Forms* | forms operating across culture and politics through wholes, rhythms, hierarchies, and networks | open factor sets plus systematic inspectable records and source custody |
| knowledge graphs and semantic networks | entities or concepts connected by typed relations | method-owned factorization, boundaries, alternatives, review, and declared loss |

Primary-source quotations and source-quality notes are preserved in
`FORMOGRAPHY-PRIMARY-SOURCE-PACKET.md`.

## Proposed object model

| Object | Working meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| form | organization of factors and relations under a declared frame | not a metaphysical universal |
| factor | selected constituent or coordinate in one view | not automatically independent or causal |
| relation | typed, directed, scoped correspondence or dependency | not inferred from co-occurrence |
| frame | question, purpose, perspective, context, scale, and version | does not become a canonical concept by default |
| formograph | one bounded, inspectable map | not necessarily a Factor Table |
| projection | a loss-declared view of a formograph for one use | not a replacement authority |
| Formarium | a curated reference collection produced through the method | proposed publication name only |
| Structure Atlas | a reader-facing comparative projection across selected formographs | not the canonical collection or the field |

## V0 boundaries

Formography V0 would:

- map declared concepts, systems, quantities, decisions, or practices;
- admit multiple senses and alternative factorizations;
- type important relations and preserve their direction and qualifiers;
- expose context, boundary, evidence, maturity, and unresolved state;
- declare projection loss and stopping conditions;
- remain compatible with human-readable Tables and machine-readable graphs.

A Formarium and a Structure Atlas are deliberately different consequences. A
Formarium would retain curated canonical records and their custody. A
Structure Atlas would select and arrange loss-declared comparative projections
for navigation, teaching, or cross-domain reading.

Formography V0 would not:

- claim one universal ontology or natural decomposition;
- infer truth, causation, equivalence, or hierarchy from proximity;
- replace domain authority, empirical methods, or professional judgment;
- require Factorium as its only implementation;
- define a general-purpose knowledge representation language;
- establish novelty from terminology alone.

## Deletion and simplification target

A successful field contract should replace repeated ad hoc explanations of
“factoring plus graph plus context plus provenance” with one source-audited
method definition and comparison boundary. If it adds vocabulary without
deleting explanatory ambiguity, the proposal fails its simplification test.

## Disproof conditions

1. An established field already combines the full V0 contract under a stable
   name and method.
2. `Formography` has a conflicting established meaning that would predictably
   mislead the intended audience.
3. Independent coders cannot distinguish forms, factors, relations, frames,
   and projections in representative cases.
4. The method cannot produce a useful artifact beyond ordinary concept maps,
   knowledge graphs, ontologies, or faceted classifications.
5. Cross-domain use requires silently changing the core terms.
6. The method's claimed integrity boundaries cannot be mechanically or
   editorially checked.

## Required narrowing before publication

1. Name and contrast the Lippert/Kühnen/Wagenknecht formography directly.
2. Treat Ranganathan's analytico-synthetic facet work as a founding precedent,
   not as a competitor to dismiss.
3. Demonstrate substantive output beyond ISO Topic Maps and ordinary property
   graphs rather than relying on vocabulary differences.
4. Show that source custody, alternatives, boundaries, and projection loss
   change at least one result in representative cases.
5. Keep `proposed field` and `candidate method` language until independent
   coding and cross-domain tests survive.
