# Formography Paper Program

Status: proposed sequence; prior-art gate open

## Paper 1 - Defining Formography

Working title: **Formography: Mapping Forms, Factors, and Relations**

Purpose:

- define the proposed field and its object;
- separate method, artifact, publication, and implementation;
- state falsification and novelty boundaries;
- compare the minimum contract with neighboring disciplines.

Gate: exact-term and prior-art module reaches a reviewed `PROCEED` decision.

## Paper 2 - The Formographic Map

Working title: **The Formographic Map: Context, Projection, and
Non-Equivalence**

Purpose:

- formalize forms, factors, typed relations, frames, and projections;
- define direction, scope, version, evidence, and loss;
- distinguish graph representation from method authority;
- provide accepted and rejected examples.

Gate: field contract and comparative encoding packet are stable.

## Paper 3 - Neighboring Disciplines

Working title: **Formography Among Concept Maps, Knowledge Graphs,
Ontologies, and Reference Systems**

Purpose:

- compare semantics rather than vocabulary;
- identify reused, adapted, and rejected precedents;
- narrow any novelty claim to the surviving method contract;
- record negative results and collisions.

Gate: authoritative sources and comparator artifacts are reconstructable.

## Paper 4 - From Formographs to the Formarium

Working title: **From Formographs to the Formarium: A Reference Architecture
for Forms and Relations**

Purpose:

- define curated collection and publication rules;
- retain canonical identity, provenance, review, and versioning;
- separate Tables, Reader, guides, concordances, and software;
- show that Formarium is one realization rather than the field itself;
- specify a Structure Atlas as a loss-declared reader projection across
  selected formographs rather than a second canonical authority.

Gate: method remains implementable outside Factorium.

## Paper 5 - Formography in Practice

Working title: **Formography in Practice: Cross-Domain Mapping Without a
Universal Ontology**

Purpose:

- apply one protocol across three or more domains;
- report independent coding, disagreements, and failures;
- test whether typed relations and projection loss prevent mistakes;
- separate technical repeatability from reader usefulness.

Gate: frozen cases, independent coding, and external reader protocol.

## Publication boundary

No paper may describe Formography as a new field until the prior-art review and
method comparison survive. Until then, use **proposed field**, **candidate
method**, and **working definition**.
