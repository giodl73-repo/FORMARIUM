# Factorium Roadmap

Status: active

Current milestones: **R4 - Volume prototype evidence** and
**R5 - First substantial volume**

Current execution milestones: **R4E - The Sieve** and
**R5A - Factor Forge intake**

Completed: **R0 - Reference foundation**, **R1 - Editorial pilot**,
**R2 - Reproducible reference**, **R3 - Guides and mappings**

Factorium grows in reviewed layers: first prove the editorial form, then make
the reference reproducible, then publish a coherent volume, and only then
scale software and contributors. Milestones are gate-driven rather than tied
to speculative calendar dates.

## North-star outcome

A reader can look up a concept, select the intended sense, compare credible
structures or relations, see decisive constraints and failure signs, and
follow every important factor to another reviewed entry or visible unresolved
candidate.

The enduring product is a linked reference and its books. Software remains a
supporting publication and navigation system.

## Current baseline

Factorium already has:

- a Root Table and a general factor-role vocabulary;
- a typed entry, sense, and specialized-view architecture;
- eleven bounded reference table families;
- Reference Delta comparisons against dictionaries, thesauri,
  encyclopedias, handbooks, and standards;
- twenty-six substantial anchor entries spanning quantities, science,
  information, economics, identity, lifecycle, policy, publication,
  diagnostics, physical constants, evidence, systems, organizations, control,
  and software;
- Formula Tables covering every V0 relation kind at pilot depth;
- thirty-eight specialized views across all eleven reference table families;
- a canonical 26-entry, 159-sense, 256-factor, 38-view interchange corpus;
- deterministic generated catalogs and unresolved-candidate reporting;
- a six-paradigm, 66-row role-to-mechanism Mapping view;
- two independently reviewed Factor Guides in software security and
  laboratory measurement;
- fixed-point `.roles` review, cited research, and immutable founding evidence.

This is a balanced architectural and editorial pilot, not yet a publishable
first volume.

## R1 - Complete the editorial pilot

### Significant quantity clusters

Build the next clusters as connected conceptual slices, not isolated formula
lists:

1. count, amount, concentration, and composition;
2. frequency, period, wavelength, phase, and wave speed;
3. charge, current, voltage, resistance, electrical energy, and power;
4. information, data, signal, noise, and signal-to-noise measures;
5. cost, price, value, utility, return, and discounting.

Each cluster should include an anchor entry, appropriate specialized views,
Reference Delta, cited research, cross-references, and fixed-point review.

### Structural and applied breadth

Balance the scientific pilots with entries that test Factorium's original
decomposition promise:

- identity, classification, naming, and versioning;
- component, capability, interface, dependency, and boundary;
- state, transition, event, process, and lifecycle;
- policy, rule, constraint, decision, and exception;
- organization, role, responsibility, authority, and accountability;
- evidence, claim, observation, measurement, and inference.

### Table-family coverage

Prototype at least one strong entry for every reference family. Mapping Tables
already have a pilot; the next priority is Decision, Transition, Constraint,
Procedure, Diagnostic, Scale, Reference Value, and Evidence Tables.

### R1 gate

- 25-50 reviewed canonical entries with no concentration in one domain;
- every substantial entry has a stable headword, separated senses, provenance,
  maturity, failure signs, and graph-resolving cross-references;
- every specialized view declares exactly one primary table-family kind;
- at least three non-scientific anchor clusters test structural decomposition;
- all critical and major `.roles` findings are closed.

## R2 - Make the reference reproducible

Freeze a small canonical interchange representation for entries, senses,
views, factors, relations, sources, maturity, aliases, and unresolved
candidates. Markdown remains the publication form, but generated projections
replace copied catalogs and counts.

Deliver:

- stable entry and view identifiers;
- machine-checkable graph links and primary-family ownership;
- schema validation for required fields and source custody;
- generated table indexes, formula catalogs, and unresolved-candidate reports;
- deterministic Markdown or book projections;
- migration of pilots without changing their editorial meaning.

R2 is complete when the canonical corpus round-trips deterministically and a
stale hand-edited index can no longer silently disagree with the entry graph.

## R3 - Prove mappings and Factor Guides

Create cross-paradigm assignment tables for object-oriented design, Rust,
functional programming, relational databases, cloud systems, and
organizational design. The mappings must show many-to-many assignments and
non-equivalences rather than redefine general factor roles.

Then define the Factor Guide method:

1. select a canonical entry and sense;
2. collect local examples, constraints, expected changes, and failure cases;
3. narrow credible alternatives;
4. record rejected alternatives and unresolved choices;
5. map selected factors to available mechanisms;
6. publish a traceable, reviewable guide.

The gate is two independently reviewed guides in materially different domains,
with every recommendation traceable to canonical entries and local evidence.

## R4 - Publish and test a volume prototype

Curate a coherent reading path rather than dumping the whole graph. A
prototype may contain roughly 75-125 selected entries and views while the
first substantial volume continues toward approximately 250 entries.

Test whether readers can:

- locate the relevant sense within minutes;
- distinguish nearby concepts and competing decompositions;
- choose the correct formula or specialized table from its scope;
- identify invalid combinations and failure signs;
- follow a factor into deeper entries;
- apply an entry to a realistic problem without author intervention.

Revise selection procedures, entry lengths, visual conventions, index paths,
and unresolved-candidate handling from observed failures.

### R4E - The Sieve

The 78-record prototype is already assembled. The next execution milestone is
the external reader round:

1. recruit 3-5 readers across at least two practitioner backgrounds;
2. run the neutral task packet without showing the evaluator rubric;
3. record de-identified task paths, time, assistance, errors, and quotes;
4. close repeated critical blockers and disposition failed or assisted tasks;
5. rerun affected tasks after critical or major revisions;
6. publish the observed R4 gate decision.

The Sieve is complete only from observed use. Author or AI simulation cannot
substitute for reader evidence.

The Sieve gates the claim that the prototype is usability-tested; it does not
freeze unrelated editorial work. The `sieve-01` test baseline remains fixed at
commit `817e779` and tag `sieve-01-prototype` while new canonical entries are
developed separately for R5. Findings from the frozen prototype must be
forward-applied deliberately rather than silently rewriting its evidence.

## R5 - First substantial volume

Publish approximately 250 reviewed entries as a coherent reference volume.
Coverage should include foundational structure, quantities and measures,
systems and change, decisions and controls, information and evidence,
organizations, and software mechanisms.

The publication gate requires:

- domain-source review for every authoritative claim;
- complete citation and revision custody;
- generated navigation and cross-reference integrity;
- a visible maturity model;
- usability evidence from the volume prototype;
- no unresolved critical or major role finding.

R5 editorial batches may begin while R4 reader sessions are being scheduled.
This concurrency prevents external availability from idling the repository,
but it does not waive either gate: R4 cannot close without observed use, and
R5 cannot publish until relevant Sieve findings are dispositioned against the
expanded volume.

## R6 - Factorium Workbench

Only after the editorial contract is stable, build software for:

- faceted search by headword, sense, factor role, family, domain, and maturity;
- side-by-side comparison of decompositions and formulas;
- graph navigation and unresolved-candidate queues;
- guided construction and review of entries;
- narrowing canonical tables into Factor Guides;
- citation, revision, and publication workflows.

AI may propose candidates, alternatives, links, and missing constraints.
Nothing becomes authoritative without visible source and human review.

## Ongoing workstreams

| Workstream | Continuing obligation |
|---|---|
| Editorial quality | Preserve sense separation, concise tables, alternatives, constraints, and failure signs |
| Domain authority | Cite appropriate standards, textbooks, handbooks, or primary sources |
| Graph integrity | Resolve factors and cross-references without inventing duplicate headwords |
| Role review | Run the relevant `.roles` panel to fixed point before publication |
| Reader evidence | Record lookup failures and revise the reference form, not just individual prose |
| Founding evidence | Keep FACTOR packets immutable and claims narrow |
| Scope control | Reject unreviewed bulk generation and premature framework work |
