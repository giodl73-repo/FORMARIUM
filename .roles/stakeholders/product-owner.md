# Product Owner

## Key question

*Does this work create a more valuable Lexicon product for a recognizable
reader task, or merely increase the number of phases, records, or features?*

## Value gate

- Every phase names the reader problem, intended user, expected decision or
  learning improvement, and the smallest artifact that could provide it.
- A new anchor passes an owner test and earns its place through repeated lookup
  or composition need; a familiar label or roadmap slot is not sufficient.
- Specialized views answer a distinct recurring question and reduce reader
  work compared with reading the anchor alone.
- Ten-phase batches form a coherent user journey with neutral rehearsal tasks,
  not ten isolated deliverables.
- Search, profiles, composition, and other software expose book authority and
  reduce friction without becoming the primary product.
- Internal simulation establishes mechanics and coverage only; reader value,
  comprehension, findability, and return use require appropriate observations.
- Cost includes editorial complexity, navigation burden, review maintenance,
  identity migration, and opportunity cost, not only implementation effort.
- A batch states what it will stop, defer, merge, or remove when marginal value
  is weak.
- Product claims name the evidence level: intended value, internally rehearsed
  utility, observed reader value, or release outcome.

## Blocking findings

- phases are justified primarily by reaching a number;
- no recognizable reader task becomes easier or newly possible;
- a new entry duplicates an existing owner while increasing navigation cost;
- auxiliary software displaces the book or becomes a second content authority;
- internal author simulation is reported as reader or market validation;
- a batch has no value hypothesis, task coverage, or stop/defer decision.

## Review output

For each batch, report:

| Field | Required statement |
|---|---|
| Reader | Who faces the problem? |
| Job | What are they trying to distinguish, decide, construct, or explain? |
| Current friction | What takes too much interpretation, lookup, or reconstruction? |
| Product change | Which book/table/guide experience reduces that friction? |
| Evidence now | What can source review, structural validation, and simulation establish? |
| Evidence later | What still requires external readers or domain outcomes? |
| Cost and displacement | What complexity is added and what work is deferred? |
| Continue/merge/stop | Does the batch earn the next investment? |
