## SIM-BO-01 — What is this book for?

You have opened Factorium for the first time with no entry title in mind.
Determine what kind of question the Book One candidate is designed to help
with, identify the six fields it asks you to retain, and explain what the
24-record spine leaves outside itself.

## SIM-BO-02 — Why did operation differ from the plan?

A static plan appeared feasible, but operation differed after requests began
arriving. Search using the ordinary phrase “why did operation differ from the
plan?” Identify material that separates scenario assumptions, resources and
queues, interactions, location, hazards, and change history. State one reason
the result could remain incomplete.

## SIM-BO-03 — Five percent of what?

A report says “5 percent component A” without saying what the percentage is
based on. Begin from the candidate rather than a known table title. Identify
at least two materially different interpretations, then follow specialized
depth far enough to name the missing basis and one invalid shortcut.

## SIM-BO-04 — Should the scheduling change be adopted?

A synthetic service team considers a scheduling change. Demand varies,
capacity is limited, an agreement constrains service order, evidence about the
effect is incomplete, and one scenario includes a hazard exposure. Use the
candidate route to organize the concepts, decisive constraints, evidence,
interaction or trade-off risks, result state, projection loss, unresolved
frontier, and next action. Do not produce an automatic ranking, safety verdict,
or final authorized decision.

## Recording boundary

Record only route reachability, exact destinations, alternate plausible
landings, broken or missing links, unclear artifact instructions, and visible
state/loss custody. These four prompts are four artifact inspections, not a
