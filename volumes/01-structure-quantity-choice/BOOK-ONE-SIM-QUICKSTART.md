# Book One Candidate Quickstart

Status: internal `sim-30` candidate surface; not `preview-01` or reader evidence

## Bring one bounded question

Factorium helps when one familiar label hides different meanings, when a
decision depends on several interacting concepts, or when an answer needs its
constraints and unresolved parts kept visible.

Begin with the [Bounded-Question Composition and Evaluation Guide](../../guides/bounded-question-composition-book-one.md).
Its 24-record spine is a teaching route through the larger reference. The
other 151 canonical records remain searchable specialized depth; they were
not deleted, deprecated, or judged less authoritative.

## Write the six-field brief

Before browsing, write:

1. **Question** — the exact distinction, comparison, decision, or explanation.
2. **Working concepts** — words or concepts you already expect to matter.
3. **Decisive constraints** — what could invalidate an attractive answer.
4. **Result state** — complete, incomplete, contradictory, or truncated.
5. **Unresolved frontier** — the next missing concept, evidence, condition, or
   authority.
6. **Next action** — inspect, obtain evidence, revise context, reconcile,
   defer, or decide under named authority.

You may not know fields 2-6 yet. Leaving them unresolved is better than
guessing.

## Choose a start

- **Question first:** use the candidate guide for the full bounded route.
- **Search first:** enter ordinary terms; internal IDs are never required.
- **Contents first:** follow the five-part candidate spine, then branch into
  specialized depth only when the question requires it.
- **Compose first:** use the bounded Composition Lab only when several selected
  concepts and reviewed relations are already known. Problem prose does not
  create graph edges.

An ordinary search such as “why did operation differ from the plan?” should
lead toward the candidate guide and operating-condition material. Search
ranking is navigation assistance, not semantic authority.

## Read the result honestly

- `complete` means the exact declared structural contract has no recorded
  frontier, conflict, failed check, or unresolved check. It is not universal
  truth.
- `incomplete` retains missing evidence or concepts.
- `contradictory` retains an explicit conflict instead of flattening it away.
- `truncated` shows that a finite budget stopped expansion.

Every flattened answer must keep decisive constraints, unresolved frontier,
declared loss, provenance, and limitations available in every reading profile.
