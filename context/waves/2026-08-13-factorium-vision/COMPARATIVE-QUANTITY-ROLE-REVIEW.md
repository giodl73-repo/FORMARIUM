## Findings and closure

| ID | Severity | Finding | Closure |
|---|---|---|---|
| CQF-001 | critical | Numerator and denominator could be reversed without notice. | Closed: comparison order and role names are mandatory. |
| CQF-002 | critical | Unlike units could be cancelled as though every quotient were dimensionless. | Closed: ratio and rate unit behavior are distinct. |
| CQF-003 | major | Unit rate could be treated as an independent measurement. | Closed: it is an equivalent normalized view. |
| CQF-004 | critical | Cross multiplication could hide mismatched quantity roles. | Closed: correspondence and nonzero denominators precede algebra. |
| CQF-005 | major | Percentage could be reported without its base. | Closed: base, subset interpretation, and display scale are required. |
| CQF-006 | critical | Relative change could use an unstated or reversed baseline. | Closed: old value owns the denominator by default and direction is explicit. |
| CQF-007 | critical | Zero-baseline relative change could receive a fabricated finite value. | Closed: the ordinary relation is undefined at zero baseline. |
| CQF-008 | critical | Percent change and percentage-point difference could be merged. | Closed: separate formulas and the 10%-to-15% diagnostic expose both answers. |
| CQF-009 | major | Every percentage could be forced into a bounded part-whole interpretation. | Closed: subset-bounded and general scaled-comparison uses are distinct. |
| CQF-010 | major | Intermediate rounding could change the reported comparison. | Closed: rounding occurs after the governing relation and precision is declared. |

No critical or major finding remains open for candidate publication.
