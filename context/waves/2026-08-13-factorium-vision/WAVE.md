# Wave: Factorium Vision and Reference Foundation

## Goal

Reorient the repository around Factorium as a lasting encyclopedia of
decomposition patterns, with books and Factor Tables as the primary
deliverables and software, experiments, and evidence as supporting layers.

## Thesis

People repeatedly need to discover dimensions, choose pivots, separate senses,
assign supporting factors to available mechanisms, and recognize invalid
combinations. Existing knowledge is scattered across disciplines and usually
presented as prose or local convention.

Factorium can become a new kind of reference: concise, table-first, plural
about credible alternatives, and disciplined about evidence and uncertainty.

## Deliverable order

1. books and Factor Tables;
2. narrowed Factor Guides;
3. reviews, evidence, and provenance;
4. search and construction software;
5. experiments and executable demonstrations.

## Success criteria

- a practitioner can find a relevant sense and alternatives within minutes;
- entries distinguish polysemy from competing decompositions;
- pivots and supporting factor roles are explicit;
- constraints prevent misleading Cartesian products;
- platform mappings follow general roles rather than define them;
- maturity and provenance remain visible without overwhelming the tables;
- existing FACTOR packets remain immutable founding evidence;
- software remains auxiliary to the reference;
- `.roles` has no unresolved critical or major finding.

## Scale baseline

Competitive research sets provisional targets of 25–50 pilot entries, about
250 entries for the first volume, about 1,000 core headwords, and 3,000–5,000
only for a mature reviewed reference. Standard entries target 250–600 words.
See `docs/research/2026-08-13-reference-scale-and-entry-size.md`.

## Entry graph baseline

Factorium is maintained as a typed graph and published through book, hierarchy,
index, and search projections. Factor count and recursive depth remain
separate; prime/composite roles are view-relative; every published factor
resolves to a canonical entry or visible candidate. The first graph example is
an eight-factor access-control request under the broader security entry.
