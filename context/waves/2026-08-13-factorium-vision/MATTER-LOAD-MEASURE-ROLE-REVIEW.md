## Findings and closure

| ID | Severity | Finding | Closure |
|---|---|---|---|
| MLF-001 | critical | Mass and weight could be collapsed by ordinary usage. | Closed: they have separate senses, quantity kinds, units, dimensions, and a gravitational-context relation. |
| MLF-002 | critical | Weight could be treated as invariant across locations. | Closed: local free-fall acceleration and reference context are required factors. |
| MLF-003 | major | A kilogram display could be assumed to prove direct mass sensing. | Closed: sensor response, inferred quantity, calibration, and display label are separated. |
| MLF-004 | critical | Density could be inferred from mass without occupied volume. | Closed: shared sample boundary, volume, and averaging scope are mandatory. |
| MLF-005 | major | Bulk density could be presented as a uniform local property. | Closed: average and local-field views are distinct. |
| MLF-006 | critical | Pressure could use total force regardless of direction. | Closed: only the force component normal to the selected area enters the scalar relation. |
| MLF-007 | critical | Pressure could be computed without matching loaded area. | Closed: force, area, boundary, and distribution scope must correspond. |
| MLF-008 | major | Pressure could be treated as a vector. | Closed: pressure is scalar while resulting surface force has a normal direction. |
| MLF-009 | major | Pressure could silently replace the broader mechanics of stress. | Closed: stress is an explicit contrast and specialist follow-on entry. |
| MLF-010 | critical | Formula rearrangements could erase zero-denominator and model restrictions. | Closed: inverse forms name nonzero denominators and applicable model conditions. |

No critical or major finding remains open for candidate publication.
