Six queries permit a numeric web-search/Wikipedia comparison, and Factorium is
within one additional open on all six. On the other six, neither comparison
surface exposes frozen decision structure within budget while Factorium does;
these are recorded as `factorium-only-within-budget`, not converted from null
to zero. Together the frozen route rule passes 12/12. This remains interaction
mechanics, not reader speed or findability.

The direct observation pass and hash-shuffled replay pass score 72 rows each.
Four of 648 binary observations disagreed. Capture-grounded reconciliation
retains multiple Factorium family meanings for `field`, retains Wikipedia's
visible risk dimensions, and rejects a governing-factor interpretation of the
web-search Cochrane evidence snippet. No disagreement changes the failed
Merriam-Webster gate.

  Factorium exposes decision structure, but its first family is specialized

## Product decision

The result rejects “Factorium has decision structure where lexical references
do not” as a differentiating claim. The next competitive hypothesis should be
narrower: on the six parity queries, does Factorium expose **consequential
depth**—governing fields, invalid transfers, stopping conditions, and a usable
handoff—rather than merely one contrast? That test must freeze task prompts and
separate each output dimension; CAD-01 must remain failed.

## Evidence boundary

This is a same-evaluator internal output audit. Generic web-search results are
a reproducible status-quo substitute, Merriam-Webster was captured through a
bounded text proxy, and the OneLook comparison uses its documented Datamuse
core rather than the dynamic interface. The result establishes neither reader
recognition, comprehension, preference, usefulness, trust, adoption, market
position, uniqueness, nor general product superiority.
