# Trade-off, Compensation, Dominance, and Conflict Decision Table

Status: candidate Decision Table

Primary family: Decision Table

Canonical headword: [Assumption, Condition, Scenario, Case, and Feasibility](../entries/scenario-assumption-condition-case.md)

Canonical senses: `scenario`, `feasibility`, `sufficiency`

## Governing question

Across declared scenarios, when do alternatives trade gains for losses,
dominate, conflict, compensate, or remain incomparable?

## Decision table

| Relation | Required declaration | Valid conclusion | Frequent misuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| trade-off | alternatives, criteria, directions, gains/losses, scenario, preference holder | one gain accompanies another loss | one universal exchange rate |
| compensation | loss/deficit, compensating gain/action, rule, authority, limits | deficit may be offset under declared rule | hard constraint or harm averaged away |
| dominance | alternative set, criteria, directions, uncertainty, ties, scenario | no worse on all declared criteria and better on at least one under rule | global superiority outside frame |
| conflict | exact requirements, resources, actions, claims, or values that cannot jointly hold | joint satisfaction is invalid or unresolved | mere disagreement or low score |
| incomparability | missing common basis, partial preference, unresolved evidence, or invalid mapping | no justified ordering under current record | equality or tie |

## Review sequence

1. Freeze alternative, scenario, population, horizon, criteria, directions,
   scales, evidence, and uncertainty.
2. Apply eligibility/admissibility and hard feasibility constraints before
   compensatory comparison.
3. Record criterion-level results and interactions; retain missing and
   incomparable cells.
4. Declare preference holder, compensation rule, vetoes, and authority.
5. Test dominance under uncertainty and across material scenarios.
6. Report retained set, conflict, or incomparability separately from
   recommendation and selection.

## Constraints

- Compensation is rule- and authority-bound, not a universal property.
- Hard constraints, rights, incompatible units, and vetoes are not averaged
  away without an explicit authorized exception.
- Dominance is relative to declared criteria, directions, scenario, and
  uncertainty.
- Conflict can concern requirements, resources, timing, interfaces, claims, or
  values; the exact kind remains visible.
- A trade-off record does not select an alternative.
