# Criterion, Preference, and Priority Decision Table

Status: candidate Decision Table

Primary family: Decision Table

Canonical headword: [Evaluation Measure, Scale, Criterion, and Score](../entries/evaluation-measure-scale-criterion.md)

Canonical senses: `criterion`, `evaluation-frame`, `evaluation-result`

## Governing question

How should relevance, preference, weight, priority, and importance be recorded
without turning unlike judgments into one unexplained number?

## Decision table

| Concept | Governing question | Required record | Must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| criterion | What feature or rule is relevant to this judgment? | definition, scope, evidence, direction, authority | measurement method or threshold automatically |
| preference | Which admissible alternative/state is favored by whom and under what conditions? | holder, pair/set, strength/form, conditions, revision | universal value or hard constraint |
| weight | Which declared combination role changes a component's influence? | weight semantics, basis, normalization, range, elicitation/source | causal effect, probability, evidence quality, or moral worth |
| priority | What should receive earlier attention or precedence under which resource/urgency rule? | subject, rule, horizon, ties, override, authority | enduring importance or selection |
| importance | In which exact sense does a factor matter to a result, stakeholder, model, or consequence? | importance sense, result, method, comparison, uncertainty | one universal scalar property |

## Gate sequence

1. Separate eligibility and hard constraints from criteria used among feasible
   alternatives.
2. Define each criterion's subject, measure or assessment method, scale,
   direction, evidence, and missingness.
3. Record whose preferences apply, how they were obtained, and when they may
   change.
4. Name the semantics of every weight before combining anything.
5. Preserve noncompensatory rules, interactions, vetoes, ties, contested
   criteria, and unresolved values.
6. Report criterion-level results beside any aggregate recommendation.

## Constraints

- A criterion can matter without being measurable on one numeric scale.
- Preference does not override feasibility, evidence, rights, or authority.
- Weighting does not make component values commensurate.
- Priority can change with horizon, capacity, urgency, dependency, or policy.
- Recommendation and final decision remain distinct and retain authority.
