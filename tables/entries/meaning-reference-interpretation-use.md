# Meaning, Reference, Interpretation, and Use

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Meaning asks what an expression, sign, act, representation, or work is
understood to convey in a stated context. Reference asks what it is used to
pick out or range over. Interpretation names the accountable activity and
result of arriving at a reading. Use locates an occurrence among participants,
purposes, audiences, conventions, and circumstances. These roles interact,
but none can silently stand for the others.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `semantic-meaning` | What content is this expression, sign, act, or work understood to convey here? | interpreted content |
| `reference` | What entity, class, event, property, relation, or possibility is it used to pick out or range over? | referential target or domain |
| `interpretation-act` | Who applies which language, convention, method, evidence, and purpose to understand the subject? | accountable activity |
| `interpretation-result` | Which reading was reached, with what scope, alternatives, loss, and confidence? | revisable result |
| `use-context` | How is the expression or practice used by which participants, for which purpose and audience? | situated occurrence |
| `ambiguity` | Which materially different admissible senses or references remain unresolved? | retained semantic alternatives |
| `translation` | How is content projected between languages or symbol systems, in which direction and with which loss? | mediated mapping |

## Root factorization

```text
meaning-reference-interpretation-use
  := expression, sign, act, work, or practice identity
   x source form, language, version, and medium
   x candidate sense and reference domain
   x speaker, author, performer, or producer position
   x interpreter and interpretive activity
   x use context, participants, purpose, and audience
   x convention, co-text, situation, genre, and history
   x evidence, inference, assumptions, and defeaters
   x ambiguity, alternatives, disagreement, and unresolved cases
   x translation direction, mediator, correspondence, and loss
   x authority, restriction, provenance, review, and revision
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| semantic meaning vs. semantic information | both concern content in context | what something conveys vs. that content considered as communicated, received, or known for a task |
| meaning vs. reference | an expression can participate in both | conveyed or interpreted content vs. what is picked out or ranged over |
| meaning vs. speaker intent | intent can guide interpretation | interpretable content under language and use vs. one producer's purpose or intended effect |
| interpretation vs. decoding | both can transform a source | evidence- and convention-governed selection of meaning vs. application of a representation mapping |
| interpretation vs. inference | either can support the other | assigning or arguing for meaning vs. connecting premises to a conclusion under a rule or model |
| ambiguity vs. uncertainty | both retain alternatives | multiple admissible senses or references vs. incomplete confidence more generally |
| translation vs. exact mapping | both relate source and target | directional, mediated projection with possible loss vs. a declared structure-preserving correspondence |

## Dependencies and invalid combinations

- Meaning depends on a selected subject, context, language or convention, and
  interpretive scope; these are not independent filters.
- Reference can fail, shift, remain plural, or be disputed without making the
  expression meaningless.
- Conventional meaning, producer intent, audience uptake, practical use, and
  legal or scholarly interpretation may disagree.
- Repeated English glosses do not establish exact translation.
- Statistical similarity, co-occurrence, embedding proximity, and mutual
  information do not establish semantic equivalence.
- Access does not grant permission or standing to interpret, translate,
  publish, train on, or generalize a source. Standing and permission also do
  not make an interpretation valid; evidence and alternatives remain visible.
- Ambiguity remains visible until a declared question and context resolve it;
  the most frequent sense is not an automatic answer.

## Inclusion and stopping boundary

This anchor includes a role only when it changes how a reader distinguishes
content, referent, interpretive activity or result, situated use, semantic
alternatives, or directional translation. It stops before theories, schools,
languages, genres, interpretive methods, named concepts, translation pairs,
and domain-specific canons. Those remain sources, examples, or scoped views.
Truth, knowledge, information, identity, representation, inference, ownership,
and permission retain their existing owners.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify the exact expression, sign, act, representation, work, or practice.
2. Record form, language, version, medium, producer, and relevant context.
3. State whether the question concerns meaning, reference, interpretation,
   use, ambiguity, or translation.
4. Name the interpreter, method, conventions, evidence, assumptions, and
   purpose rather than presenting interpretation as an automatic decode.
5. Retain alternatives, disagreement, failed reference, uncertainty, and
   defeaters.
6. For translation, record source and target, direction, mediator, chosen
   correspondence, rejected alternatives, and loss.
7. Record authority, restrictions, provenance, review, and supersession.

|---|---|---|

## Failure signs

- a gloss is treated as a context-free definition;
- a name, identifier, referent, meaning, and intention share one field;
- a translation is stored without direction or loss;
- interpretation has no interpreter, method, evidence, or alternatives;
- public availability is treated as permission;
- ambiguity is converted into generic low confidence;
- one use occurrence is generalized into universal meaning.
