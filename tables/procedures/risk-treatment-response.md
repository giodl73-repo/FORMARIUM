# Prevention, Protection, Detection, Mitigation, Response, and Recovery Procedure

Status: candidate Procedure Table

Primary family: Procedure Table

Canonical headword: [Hazard, Exposure, Harm, Vulnerability, and Safety](../entries/hazard-exposure-harm-safety.md)

Canonical senses: `hazard`, `exposure`, `harm`, `vulnerability`, `safety`

## Governing question

Where in the hazard-to-harm pathway does a treatment act, and what evidence
shows implementation, operation, effectiveness, side effects, and residual risk?

## Treatment table

| Role | Acts primarily on | Required record | Does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| prevention | hazard creation/initiation or exposure opportunity | mechanism, scope, conditions, failure behavior | zero hazard |
| protection | subject/pathway susceptibility or consequence transfer | protected subject, barrier/capacity, limits, degradation | no exposure or harm |
| detection | hazard, exposure, state change, incident, or harm signal | target, coverage, sensitivity, delay, false/missed behavior | prevention or safety |
| mitigation | likelihood/severity/pathway after hazard exists | scenario, action, expected reduction, timing, uncertainty | elimination |
| response | active action during/after detected condition | trigger, authority, resources, coordination, objectives | successful outcome |
| recovery | restoration/adaptation after disruption or harm | target state/capability, time/data limits, residuals, evidence | return to identical prior state |

## Procedure

1. Frame subject, activity, hazard, exposure pathway, harm, vulnerability,
   scenario, evidence, and authority.
2. Map each treatment to its exact pathway location, mechanism, dependency,
   operating conditions, resource need, and failure behavior.
3. Separate intended design, implementation, readiness, activation, operation,
   measured effect, outcome, and residual risk.
4. Test interaction, common-cause failure, degraded modes, delayed detection,
   side effects, displacement, and recovery residuals.
5. Preserve monitoring, incidents/near misses, changes, review, and limitations.

## Boundary

This descriptive procedure does not prescribe controls, certify safety, or
replace domain professionals, regulation, or emergency procedures.
