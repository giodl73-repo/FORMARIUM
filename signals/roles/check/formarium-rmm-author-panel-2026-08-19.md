## Independent findings

### Gottlieb: necessity

**Pass:** The condensed book is materially better after removing item numbers,
type summaries, metadata summaries, and standalone chrome. The A-Z order,
headwords, running heads, and pointer parent links now do visible work.

**Finding:** The front door still contains several products' worth of
explanation: intent routing, two-book architecture, Composition Query examples,
a first journey, search comparisons, and the full contents. The page repeatedly
explains how to use the system after the first useful choice has already been
offered.

**Finding:** The book title page repeats operational explanation. Its inventory
subtitle and paragraph about expandable supplements describe the implementation
before giving the reader a reason to care.

**Closure:** Cut or move explanatory homepage sections rather than restyling
them. On the book title page, retain the title, one reader-facing promise, and
the compact edition line.

### McPhee: arrangement and restraint

**Pass:** Alphabetical order is the strongest structure in the publication.
The bounded two-column pages, running heads, and `Appears in` links let that
structure be felt rather than explained.

**Finding:** The site exposes too many simultaneous organizing systems:
task-based routes, two books, problem-led graphs, a five-step journey, chapter
parts, search families, and A-Z. Each system is defensible, but together they
make the architecture visible as machinery.

**Finding:** Canonical entries do not yet share a sufficiently quiet opening
grammar. A headword can be followed by internal maturity status before
orientation, while pointer entries move directly from gloss to cross-reference.
The reader should encounter meaning before publication state.

**Closure:** Let A-Z own the condensed book. Use a stable opening sequence of
headword, orientation or governing distinction, then core structure. Move
maturity and publication state to a less interruptive evidence location.

### Calvino: lightness and multiplicity

**Pass:** Interleaving pointers and canonical entries creates useful
multiplicity. Pointer cross-references make the reference feel explorable rather
than merely indexed.

**Finding:** The public language is continuously grave: `canonical`, `bounded`,
`typed closure`, `authority`, `projection`, and `unresolved` recur before the
reader experiences discovery. Accuracy is protected, but delight is deferred.

**Finding:** The site lacks a low-commitment route for serendipity. Every route
asks the reader to know a term, bring a question, learn a method, or understand
the architecture.

**Closure:** Add lightness by replacement, not expansion. Replace one
architecture paragraph with one surprising concrete distinction, and consider
a small `Surprise me` or random-headword route that uses the existing corpus
without creating new authority.

### Sagan: wonder and accessibility

**Pass:** Evidence boundaries are unusually honest. The site identifies
candidate maturity, preserves provenance, distinguishes projection from
authority, and avoids claiming reader outcomes.

**Finding:** The first public promise is an inventory, not an invitation:
`250 structural pointers` and `54 canonical Table families` tell the reader how
much exists but not what new understanding becomes possible.

**Finding:** A smart general reader meets specialist terms before seeing one
memorable demonstration. `Factorization`, `canonical Table family`, `typed
closure`, and `projection` require prior investment.

**Closure:** Lead with one plain-language transformation, such as showing how
one familiar word separates into several materially different senses and
structures. Keep the counts and custody in the edition line or About material.

## Synthesis

### Consensus

1. Preserve the current condensed-book mechanics: A-Z order, dense pages,
   running heads, and linked pointer parents.
2. Stop adding navigation or explanatory chrome to the condensed book.
3. Reduce the home page after its first successful route choice.
4. Replace inventory-led and architecture-led prose with one concrete,
   reader-facing demonstration.
5. Put meaning before maturity metadata in canonical entry openings.

### Productive disagreement

Gottlieb's role favors cutting while Calvino's and Sagan's roles ask for delight
and wonder. The compatible answer is **replacement, not addition**: remove one
explanatory block and use that space for one vivid example or a lightweight
serendipity action.

McPhee's role favors invisible arrangement while Sagan's role asks for clearer
explanation. The compatible answer is **demonstration, not instruction**: show
one word being usefully distinguished rather than adding another paragraph
about the method.

## Verdict

The next gains are editorial: a clearer promise, less visible architecture,
meaning before status, and one small invitation to discovery.
