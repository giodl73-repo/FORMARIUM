# Morpheme, Inflection, Derivation, Compounding, and Word Formation

## Decision question

Does Lexicon need one owner for the internal structure of words, the relation
between a lexeme and its word forms, and the processes by which new lexemes are
formed in a language?

## Scope decision

This brief closes a gap that the
[pattern brief](pattern-regularity-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md) first
recorded as an exclusion. That brief removed `morphology` from its title
because the word names several unrelated fields. Two of those fields have named
owners and are genuinely closed false gaps: shape-equivalence morphology
belongs to the canonical geometric entry, and set-theoretic image-operator
morphology — dilation, erosion, opening, and closing over structuring elements
— belongs to that same geometric owner as operations on shape.
Linguistic morphology was not closed by any owner; it was simply unowned, and
it carries a coherent, recurring, and well-documented contract. This brief
admits that field as a bounded candidate.

Urban morphology remains unowned and is recorded as open domain-specific
residue, not as a closed gap. This candidate does not attempt a universal
`form` sense of any kind.

## Bounded thesis

Proceed only as a word-internal structure entry for language. Semantic content
and interpretation, situational variation of language, generic recurrence,
generic decomposition and composition, the general-to-particular relation, and
identifier semantics retain their existing or candidate owners.

The thesis is falsified if morpheme, inflection, derivation, compounding, and
word formation can be recorded completely as generic decomposition of a string
plus recurrence without losing paradigm membership, category values, base and
head identification, productivity, or the choice of analytical model; or if
inflection and derivation cannot be separated well enough to be usable given
their documented gradience.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `lexeme-word-form` | Which lexeme is realized by which word form, in which paradigm cell? |
| `morpheme` | Which minimal meaningful unit is identified in this form, by which segmentation and identity criterion, and with which allomorphs? |
| `inflection` | Which grammatical category value is realized on a word form of the same lexeme, as required by paradigm or syntax? |
| `derivation` | Which new lexeme is formed from which base, with which category or meaning change? |
| `compounding` | Which two or more lexical bases combine into one lexeme, and which base is the head? |
| `word-formation-process` | Which non-affixal process, such as conversion, reduplication, blending, clipping, or back-formation, forms the new lexeme? |
| `productivity` | Which pattern remains available for new formations, under which restrictions and blocking conditions? |

## Candidate contract

```text
morphology-word-formation-use
  := language, variety, and period
   x word form, lexeme, and paradigm cell
   x segmentation into morphs, morphemes, and allomorphs
   x root, stem, base, and affix identification
   x process type of affixation, compounding, conversion, reduplication, blending, or clipping
   x grammatical categories, features, and values realized
   x category or meaning change relative to the base
   x head, headedness, and transparency for compounds
   x productivity, restrictions, blocking, and gaps
   x lexicalization, listedness, and irregularity
   x analysis basis of morpheme-based or word-and-paradigm, with evidence
   x language sample, data source, and glossing convention
```

## Existing-owner audit

- [Meaning, Reference, Interpretation, and Use](../../../tables/entries/meaning-reference-interpretation-use.md)
  owns semantic meaning, reference, interpretation, ambiguity, and translation,
  and its stopping boundary excludes languages as such. This candidate owns
  word-internal structure and formation processes, not what a word means in a
  context.
- [Decomposition Modes and Combination Contracts](../../../tables/entries/decomposition-modes-combination-contracts.md)
  owns generic decomposition and combination. Morphological segmentation is a
  language-specific analysis with its own criteria and must not be presented as
  a generic decomposition result.
- [System Composition, Architecture, Capability, Interface, and
  Dependency](../../../tables/entries/system-composition-dependency.md) owns
  generic structural composition of a whole.
- [Pattern, Regularity, Motif, Template, and Pattern
  Formation](../candidates/pattern-regularity-motif-template-pattern-formation.md)
  owns cross-instance recurrence and the artifact check; a claimed
  morphological regularity routes its recurrence evidence there.
- [Register, Style, Genre, Variety, and
  Idiolect](../candidates/register-style-genre-variety-idiolect.md) owns which
  forms are selected in a situation of use; this candidate owns how the forms
  are built.
- [Type, Token, Instantiation, Realization, and
  Embodiment](../candidates/type-token-instantiation-realization-embodiment.md)
  owns the general-to-particular relation itself. Lexeme and word form are a
  language-specific case that cites, rather than restates, that relation.
- [Identity, Namespace, Naming, Classification, and Versioning](../../../tables/entries/identity-naming-classification-versioning.md)
  owns names and identifiers; a lexeme is not an identifier.
- [Information, Data, Record, Schema, Signal, and Noise](../../../tables/entries/information-data-signal-noise.md)
  owns text encoding; a morph is not a character sequence by definition.

## Counterevidence and limits

- The inflection and derivation boundary is gradient. Relevance, generality,
  category change, paradigm obligatoriness, and productivity often disagree, so
  the criterion used must be recorded with the claim.
- Segmentation into morphemes fails or becomes arbitrary for portmanteau,
  zero-marked, subtractive, root-and-pattern, and templatic forms; some models
  reject the morpheme as a stored unit altogether.
- What counts as a word, and therefore as a compound, varies across languages
  and across criteria within one language.
- Productivity is graded and changes over time; a formation pattern is not
  simply available or unavailable.
- Typological surveys report coded values over a language sample; a value is
  not a universal claim about the language family or about language in general.
- Glossing conventions are notation and do not settle any analytical dispute.
- Historical origin of a form does not determine its current analysis.

## Admission gates

1. Exclude shape-equivalence morphology, set-theoretic image-operator
   morphology, urban morphology, and Goethean morphology by name.
2. Exclude the further homonyms of the family's own sense labels by name:
   inflection as a curve's change of concavity, which belongs to the geometric
   owner; compounding as compound interest and as chemical compound formation;
   derivation as a proof or as unit derivation in a quantity system; and
   productivity as an economic output-per-input ratio.
3. Record the language, variety, and period for every claim.
4. Separate lexeme from word form before analyzing inflection or derivation.
5. State the inflection and derivation criterion in use, and record gradient or
   conflicting outcomes rather than forcing a binary.
6. Identify base, affix, and head explicitly for derivation and compounding.
7. Record productivity, restrictions, blocking, and lexicalization as factors.
8. Record the analysis basis, morpheme-based or word-and-paradigm, and the
   evidence for stated boundaries.
9. Route generic decomposition, generic recurrence, situational variation, the
   general-to-particular relation, and identifier semantics to their owners.
10. Stop before individual languages, named grammars, and prescriptive usage.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.** The contract is
coherent and directly sourced, but the gradient inflection and derivation
boundary and the contested status of the morpheme require source and domain
review before canonical admission.
