# Appraisal, Emotion Episode, and Phenomenal Feeling — Candidate Brief

## Decision question

Should Lexicon distinguish concern-relative appraisal, a bounded emotion
episode, and phenomenal feeling without owning affect or mood broadly?

## Bounded thesis

Yes, provisionally. These questions are distinct but theories of their relation
are contested, so the candidate is evidence- and theory-relative.

## Proposed senses

| Sense ID | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| `affective-appraisal` | How is a situation evaluated relative to concerns, goals, norms, or coping possibilities? |
| `emotion-episode` | Which bounded episode links an eliciting situation, appraisal, action tendency, expression, and reported experience? |
| `phenomenal-feeling` | What subjective felt character is reported or otherwise evidenced in the episode? |

## Candidate contract

Record subject, context, concern, theory, episode interval, evidence modality,
report, expression, physiology, and cultural limits. Do not promote factors to
senses.

## Existing-owner audit

The stance candidate owns receiver-attributed `felt-mood` for an expression,
work, performance, or place; the clinical candidate owns mood only when it is
recorded as a clinical symptom. Evaluation owns declared evaluative frames,
motive owns goals and intentions, and causal reasoning owns mechanism. This
candidate owns neither generic affect nor generic psychological mood.

## Source matrix

| Source | Contribution | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SEP, Emotion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotion/) | Documents feeling, evaluative, and motivational theoretical disagreement. | It does not select one theory or universal emotion kinds. |
| [Beedie, Terry, and Lane DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930541000057) | Supports distinctions between emotion episodes and mood. | It does not settle culture, measurement, or causation debates. |
| [APA Dictionary](https://dictionary.apa.org/) | Provides disciplinary vocabulary checks. | Dictionary definitions are not a complete theory. |

## Admission gates

Require theory-tagged examples, scope-specific mood routing, an explicit
unowned disposition for generic psychological mood, and evidence tests that
prevent expression or category labels from standing in for feeling.

## Counterevidence and limits

Emotion categories, appraisal dimensions, and cross-cultural generalization are
actively contested. This candidate is descriptive only and offers no mental
health assessment.

## Disposition

Keep noncanonical; route attributed and clinical mood narrowly, leave generic
psychological mood unowned, and do not add affect or mood as senses.
