# Collective Action Provision and Appropriation Shortfalls

## Decision question

Does Lexicon need a bounded owner for the structural condition in which
individual non-contribution under-provides a shared benefit, or individual
extraction depletes a shared stock, when existing owners already cover
incentives, governance, access, and stock-flow accounting?

## Bounded thesis

Proceed only with the two shortfall questions. The candidate does not own
resource classes, access mechanisms, governance designs, trust, monitoring,
sanctions, group size, or payoff instruments. It adds the pre-design
collective-action condition that those owners cannot express without losing the
distinction between needing more contribution and needing less extraction.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `provision-shortfall` | Under which benefit, payoff, and access conditions does individually rational non-contribution leave a shared benefit under-provided relative to the stated collective criterion? |
| `appropriation-shortfall` | Under which stock-flow, payoff, rivalry, and access conditions does individually rational extraction deplete a shared resource stock beyond the stated sustainable or collective criterion? |

## Candidate contract

```text
collective-action-shortfall
  := group and collective criterion
   x benefit or stock-flow subject
   x excludability cost and rivalry intensity
   x contribution or extraction payoff structure
   x group size, observability, repeated interaction, and time horizon
   x free-rider condition, monitoring, sanctioning, trust, and governance where present
   x measured shortfall, evidence, uncertainty, and revision
```

## Existing-owner audit

- The incentive candidate owns deliberate payoff contingencies, externalities,
  moral hazard, and adverse selection. It does not own the pre-design
  contribution/extraction shortfall.
- Governance owns direction, obligations, assessment, and enforcement; it does
  not own the structural condition that can motivate a governance arrangement.
- The access entry owns authorization and entitlement decisions; excludability
  cost remains a resource/context factor here, not a permission sense.
- Stock-flow balance owns accounting, accumulation, and conservation; the
  appropriation sense adds strategic extraction relative to a collective
  sustainability criterion.
- Trust, institutions, monitoring, sanctions, group size, and payoff structure
  are conditions or response routes, not senses.

## Counterevidence and limits

- Voluntary contribution, assurance, and repeated interaction can avoid a
  provision shortfall without selective incentives.
- Governed common property can sustain a stock; Hardin's open-access tragedy is
  not a universal commons prediction.
- A club good can become rival at congestion and a partially excludable fishery
  can still be over-appropriated; the familiar four labels are not fixed cells.
- Team-production moral hazard routes to the incentive candidate when
  post-agreement effort change is the governing question.
- The candidate makes no claim that a shortfall determines a correct governance
  response.

## Admission gates

1. Preserve exactly two senses and keep all named labels and mechanisms as
   factors/examples.
2. Record a collective criterion, payoff structure, and relevant access/rivalry
   conditions before asserting either shortfall.
3. Preserve boundaries to incentives/externalities, institutions/governance,
   access, stock-flow, and trust.
4. Do not force a universal public/common-pool/club/private grid.
5. Remain noncanonical; no tables, relation sidecars, or canonical reference
   changes follow from this brief.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO NONCANONICAL CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.**
The two senses are distinguishable, cross-domain, and owner-bounded, while the
choice of governance or payoff remedy remains external.
