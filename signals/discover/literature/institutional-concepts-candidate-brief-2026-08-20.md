# Institutional Concepts — Candidate Brief

## Decision question

Should Lexicon describe institutional accounts and rule roles without positing
a universal social ontology?

## Bounded thesis

Yes, provisionally. The family captures distinctions among constituting,
regulating, status-bearing, and reified relations that organizational and policy
owners use but do not name at this analytical level.

## Proposed senses

| Sense ID | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| `institutional-arrangement` | Which recurring, recognized arrangement organizes roles, practices, or positions in this account? |
| `constitutive-norm` | Which rule constitutes an institutional status or activity rather than merely guiding conduct? |
| `regulative-norm` | Which rule directs conduct within a practice assumed independently of that rule? |
| `social-object-account` | Which object is treated as socially instituted under which recognition and record conditions? |
| `status-function` | Which status confers which function or power under which collective or institutional account? |
| `reified-relationship` | Which relationship is represented as an identifiable object for records or reasoning? |

## Candidate contract

Record account, source, participants, recognition evidence, rule, status,
practice, time, and contestation. A representation choice must be marked as
such.

## Existing-owner audit

`organization-role-authority` owns organizations, roles, authority, and
competency; governance and policy entries own concrete rules and decisions.
Existing relation records own relation mechanics, not this institutional
analysis.

## Admission gates

Require cross-cultural and technical-model counterexamples, source-bound
examples, and tests preventing duplication of organization or policy owners.

## Counterevidence and limits

The conditions for social objects and status functions are contested. This
candidate makes no claim about universal recognition, legitimacy, or authority.

## Disposition

Keep noncanonical and account-relative; do not promote a social-ontology theory
to a Lexicon default.
