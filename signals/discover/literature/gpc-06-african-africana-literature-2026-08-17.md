# GPC-06 African and Africana Literature Review

## Phase 1 — claims

1. Eight named packets can supply counterexamples but cannot represent Africa
   or Africana philosophy. Field surveys confirm radical plurality; language,
   region, North/East/Central Africa, diaspora, gender, and living-community
   omissions directly defeat completeness.
2. Akan achieved personhood, Yorùbá orí/destiny, ubuntu/botho relation,
   Ethiopian inquiry, Africana liberation, conceptual decolonization, gender
   critique, and performed argument are distinct pivots. Specialist sources
   support them; generic communalism or ubuntu would erase the field.
3. Oral performance, proverbs, interviews with sages, treatises, novels,
   political texts, and academic argument have different authorship and
   warrant. Method studies confirm this; transcription can falsely turn
   situated performance into anonymous group belief.
4. Colonial racialization, language hierarchy, archive and translation,
   professional-philosophy gates, gendered knowledge, and diaspora shape
   custody. Critical sources support the claim; decolonization cannot itself
   certify one authentic tradition.
5. Personhood, custom, gender, reconciliation, violence, liberation, and
   community authority cannot become guidance. Historical comparison is
   supported; normative uptake requires source- and community-qualified review.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Factorium maps “African philosophy” to ubuntu-like community,
ancestral tradition, proverbs, and liberation, converts situated performance
into collective doctrine, and treats a published interpreter as owner of
language- and community-specific meaning.
