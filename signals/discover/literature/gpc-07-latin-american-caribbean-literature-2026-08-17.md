# GPC-07 Latin American and Caribbean Literature Review

## Phase 1 — claims

1. Six packets can expose counterexamples but cannot represent Latin American
   or Caribbean philosophy. Surveys establish multilingual and transregional
   breadth; Indigenous, national, Brazilian, Caribbean, gender, disciplinary,
   and living-community omissions defeat completeness.
2. Conquest testimony, authenticity, mestizaje, liberation, dependency,
   coloniality, Afro-Latin and feminist critique, creolization, opacity, and
   sociogeny are distinct pivots. “Decolonial” or “Latin American” as one
   doctrine would erase their conflict.
3. Chronicle, testimony, scholastic dispute, essay, manifesto, theology,
   sociology, philosophy, poetry, and fiction generate different warrant.
   Proposition-only extraction hides genre, speaker position, and address.
4. Conquest, slavery, plantation economies, race-making, language hierarchy,
   dependency, nation, exile, diaspora, gender, and academic location shape
   custody. A single coloniality system can itself become over-totalized.
5. Land, conversion, race, nation, liberation, violence, gender, reparation,
   opacity, and decolonization cannot become guidance. Normative uptake
   requires qualified review.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Factorium maps a Spanish-speaking “Latin American” list to
identity, oppression, liberation, coloniality, and hybridity; uses European
conquest debates as the archive; absorbs the Caribbean and Brazil; and turns
decolonial vocabulary into one universal diagnosis and prescription.
