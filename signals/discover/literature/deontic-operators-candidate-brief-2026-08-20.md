# Deontic Operators — Candidate Brief

## Decision question

Should source-relative formal operators be admitted without displacing concrete
governance, access, policy, or legal interpretation?

## Bounded thesis

Yes, provisionally. They answer whether content is required, allowed,
forbidden, optional, waived, or exempt under a declared system—not whether a
real organization will authorize or enforce it.

## Proposed senses

| Sense ID | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| `deontic-obligation` | Under which source and interpretation is the subject required to do or maintain the stated content? |
| `deontic-permission` | Under which source and interpretation is the stated content allowed? |
| `deontic-prohibition` | Under which source and interpretation is the stated content disallowed? |
| `deontic-optionality` | Under which source are both doing and omitting the stated content permitted? |
| `deontic-waiver` | Which holder relinquishes or declines enforcement of which claim or requirement under what source? |
| `deontic-exemption` | Which source removes a subject or case from an otherwise applicable requirement? |

## Candidate contract

Record source, system, scope, subject, content, time, priority, exception,
holder, authority, and unresolved conflict. No operator is asserted without
its interpretation.

## Existing-owner audit

Existing governance owns organizational obligations; access owns permissions
and authorization; policy owns rules, decisions, and exceptions. This
candidate does not own operational enforcement or legal conclusions.

## Source matrix

| Source | Contribution | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SEP, Deontic Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-deontic/) | Establishes formal distinctions and paradox/interpretation risks. | It does not provide a universal applied-governance model. |
| [W3C ODRL Vocabulary](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-vocab/) | Gives a concrete source-relative permissions/prohibitions vocabulary. | Its web-policy scope is not all formal or legal systems. |

## Admission gates

Require examples with source and conflict data, a formal-system review, and
tests that access and governance records still route correctly.

## Counterevidence and limits

Deontic logics differ on permission, contrary-to-duty cases, and exceptions.
The candidate does not advise on law, ethics, access control, or compliance.

## Disposition

Keep as noncanonical formal vocabulary; preserve source relativity.
