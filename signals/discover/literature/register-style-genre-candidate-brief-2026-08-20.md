# Register, Style, Genre, Variety, and Idiolect

## Decision question

Does Lexicon need one owner for the systematic variation of language and
expressive form across situations of use, conventional text classes, producer
preference, and user-defined varieties, separate from meaning and separate from
attributed attitude?

## Scope decision

This brief closes the half deferred by the
[stance, tone, voice, mood, and atmosphere
brief](stance-tone-atmosphere-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md). That family owns
attributed attitude and felt quality; this one owns describable variation in
the linguistic and formal features themselves.

Diction, in the sense of word choice, is not a separate sense here. Register is
defined by co-occurring lexical and grammatical features selected for a
situation of use, and style is defined by preferential feature choices, so
lexical selection is a feature-level factor inside `register`, `style`, and
`idiolect` rather than a peer sense. Genre is admitted only as the conventional
class membership question; named genres, periods, schools, and canons remain
out of scope.

## Bounded thesis

Proceed only as a variation entry over describable features. Semantic content
and interpretation, attributed tone and stance, encoding and signal quality,
classification of entities, and evaluation scoring retain their existing or
candidate owners.

The thesis is falsified if register, genre, style, variety, and idiolect can be
recorded completely as one formality scale, or as classification of a document
type, without losing situational function, co-occurring feature sets,
conventional class expectations, producer attribution, or the use-defined
against user-defined distinction; or if register and genre cannot be separated
on cited authority.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `register` | Which lexical and grammatical features co-occur because of this situation of use, and which function do they serve? |
| `genre` | Which conventional, culturally recognized class of complete texts or works, with which expected structure, does this occurrence belong to? |
| `style` | Which preferential or aesthetic feature choices distinguish this producer or work within a register? |
| `variety-dialect` | Which user-defined regional, social, or temporal variety is in play, as against a use-defined register? |
| `formality-level` | Which position on a declared formality or deference scale is selected, and by which convention? |
| `idiolect` | Which features are attributable to one individual producer rather than to a shared variety? |

## Candidate contract

```text
register-style-genre-use
  := text, utterance, work, or corpus identity
   x producer, audience, and discourse community
   x situation of use with field, tenor, and mode
   x medium, channel, and mode of production
   x observed lexical, grammatical, and formal features
   x co-occurrence dimensions and their functional interpretation
   x conventional class membership and expected structural moves
   x preferential choices attributable to producer or work
   x user-defined variety of region, social group, or period
   x declared formality or deference scale and its convention
   x individual attribution evidence and its limits
   x corpus, sample, population, and comparison baseline
```

## Existing-owner audit

- [Meaning, Reference, Interpretation, and Use](../../../tables/entries/meaning-reference-interpretation-use.md)
  owns semantic meaning, reference, interpretation, ambiguity, and translation,
  and its stopping boundary excludes genres and interpretive methods. This
  candidate must be argued as a distinct owner of describable variation, never
  as an extension of interpretation.
- [Stance, Tone, Voice, Mood, and
  Atmosphere](../candidates/stance-tone-voice-mood-atmosphere.md) owns encoded
  stance, attributed tone, narrative voice, felt mood, and atmosphere. Register
  supplies context to that family and does not re-own attitude.
- [Information, Data, Record, Schema, Signal, and Noise](../../../tables/entries/information-data-signal-noise.md)
  owns encoding, records, and signal quality.
- [Identity, Namespace, Naming, Classification, and Versioning](../../../tables/entries/identity-naming-classification-versioning.md)
  owns classification of entities; genre membership is a conventional class
  claim about texts, evidenced by feature and structure, not an identifier
  assignment.
- [Population, Sample, Estimand, Estimate, and
  Generalization](../../../tables/entries/sampling-generalization.md) owns
  whether a corpus finding generalizes; the variation-evidence guard routes
  there.
- [Evaluation Measure, Scale, Criterion, and Score](../../../tables/entries/evaluation-measure-scale-criterion.md)
  owns declared scales; formality levels are recorded with their convention and
  are not scored here.
- [Pattern, Regularity, Motif, Template, and Pattern
  Formation](../candidates/pattern-regularity-motif-template-pattern-formation.md)
  owns recurrence and the artifact check for any claimed regularity.
- [Morpheme, Inflection, Derivation, Compounding, and Word
  Formation](../candidates/morpheme-inflection-derivation-compounding-word-formation.md)
  owns word-internal structure and formation processes. This family owns which
  forms a situation of use selects; how those forms are built routes there. A
  morphological feature may be recorded here as a co-occurring feature of a
  register or style, but never analyzed here.

## Source matrix

| Source | Contribution | Boundary or challenge |
|---|---|---|
| [Biber and Conrad, *Register, Genre, and Style*](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108686136) | the operative three-way register, genre, and style distinction and the situation-features-function frame | an empirical corpus framework; other traditions define genre differently |
| Halliday and Hasan, *Language, Context, and Text*, Oxford University Press, 1989 | register as a configuration of field, tenor, and mode | one theory of language; the tripartite model is not universally adopted |
| [Biber, *Variation across Speech and Writing*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511621024) | multidimensional analysis: registers differ along co-occurring feature dimensions | dimensions are corpus- and language-specific |
| Swales, *Genre Analysis: English in Academic and Research Settings*, Cambridge University Press, 1990 | genre as a class owned by a discourse community, with communicative purpose and moves | purpose-based genre criteria are contested |
| Joos, *The Five Clocks*, 1961 | the frozen, formal, consultative, casual, and intimate formality styles | a heuristic ladder, not an empirically validated scale |
| Crystal and Davy, *Investigating English Style*, Longman, 1969 | situational dimensions of stylistic variation | pre-corpus and English-centric |
| Labov, *Sociolinguistic Patterns*, University of Pennsylvania Press, 1972 | style shifting and the user-defined against use-defined distinction | the attention-to-speech model was later challenged |
| [Bell, "Language style as audience design"](https://doi.org/10.1017/S004740450001037X) | style as responsive to audience rather than to formality alone | competes with speaker-design and identity accounts |
| Ferguson, "Dialect, register, and genre: working assumptions about conventionalization", in Biber and Finegan, eds., *Sociolinguistic Perspectives on Register*, Oxford University Press, 1994 | an explicit statement of the dialect, register, and genre boundary problem | framed as working assumptions and deliberately non-final |

## Counterevidence and limits

- Register, genre, and style are used inconsistently across traditions; some
  frameworks treat genre as a register and others reverse the containment.
- "Register" is also a vocal range, a processor storage location, and a
  record of entries; the homonyms must be excluded by name.
- Formality is not one dimension. Multidimensional evidence shows registers
  differing along several independent feature dimensions at once.
- Corpus dimensions are specific to the corpus and language that produced
  them, so a dimension label is not a universal variety.
- Idiolectal attribution is evidentially weak for short texts and is a claim
  about a population baseline, not a fingerprint.
- Variety labels carry social judgment; a variety description is not an
  evaluation of correctness, education, or capability.
- Genre membership is community-owned and historically mutable; occurrences
  routinely mix and violate expected structures.

## Admission gates

1. Exclude vocal register, processor register, and registry records by name.
2. Separate register from genre by situational function against conventional
   complete-text class.
3. Separate register from style by functional requirement against preference.
4. Separate register from variety by use-defined against user-defined.
5. Keep diction and other lexical choices as feature-level factors, not senses.
6. Require observed features and a comparison baseline for any claim.
7. Record the corpus, sample, and population, and route generalization to the
   sampling owner.
8. Keep attributed attitude with the stance and tone candidate.
9. Stop before named genres, named varieties, prescriptive correctness, and
   author attribution as forensic proof.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.** The register,
genre, and style distinctions are directly supported, but the boundary against
the canonical interpretation owner's excluded scope, and the social-judgment
risk of variety labels, must be reviewed before canonical admission.
