# Perception, Sensation, Appearance, Illusion, and Hallucination

## Decision question

Does Formarium need a subject-relative owner for sensory conversion,
perceptual organization, perceptual presentation, appearance, illusion, and
hallucination without re-owning observation evidence?

## Bounded thesis

Proceed only as a perception-family entry. Generic observation, measurement,
signal, provenance, and interpretation remain with their existing owners. An
observation record may appear only as a bridge from a perceptual episode to an
auditable report.

The thesis is falsified if the existing signal, evidence, and interpretation
entries can preserve stimulus, transduction, organization, appearance,
illusion, and hallucination without collapsing distinct layers.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `stimulus` | Which environmental or bodily event impinges on a receptor or sensor? |
| `sensation-transduction` | How is that stimulus converted into an afferent or neural signal? |
| `perceptual-organization` | How are inputs grouped, segmented, bound, or separated as figure and ground? |
| `perceptual-content` | Which object, property, or scene is perceptually presented or represented? |
| `appearance` | How does the subject experience things as seeming from this perspective and context? |
| `illusion` | Is a present stimulus or object experienced other than it is on the selected comparison basis? |
| `hallucination` | Is there a perception-like episode without a corresponding external stimulus or object? |

## Candidate contract

```text
perception-appearance-use
  := perceiver or observer
   x modality or bodily source
   x stimulus source, medium, and context
   x receptor or transducer state
   x organization cues and conditions
   x perceptual content or presentation
   x appearance or seeming
   x comparison basis for accuracy or mismatch
   x illusion or hallucination classification
   x report method, time, provenance, and reviewer
   x clinical context, functional impact, uncertainty, and exclusions
```

## Theory and clinical limits

- Common-kind, representational, sense-data, naive-realist, and disjunctivist
  accounts remain disputed.
- Hallucination is a descriptive symptom-level term, not a diagnosis.
- Hallucination must not be collapsed with delusion.
- Named disorders, treatments, modalities, and named illusions remain outside
  this candidate.

## Admission gates

1. Keep stimulus, transduction, organization, content, appearance, illusion,
   and hallucination distinct.
2. Keep generic observation, measurement, signal, provenance, and interpretation
   with their existing owners.
3. Route recorded perceptual reports to Claim and Evidence rather than creating
   a second observation or provenance owner.
4. Preserve theory pluralism.
5. State that hallucination does not establish psychotic disorder or any other
   diagnosis.
6. Include philosophy, psychology/neuroscience, observation-standard, and public
   clinical sources.
7. Stop before diagnosis, treatment, and modality-specific catalogs.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.** The distinction
set is supportable, but clinical language and observation ownership require
specialist review.
