# Stance, Tone, Voice, Mood, and Atmosphere

## Decision question

Does Lexicon need one owner for the attitude encoded in or attributed to an
expression, work, performance, or place, and for the diffuse affective quality
that a receiver reports, separate from meaning, from perceptual appearance, and
from situational language variation?

## Scope decision

The originally proposed family was *Tone, Mood, Atmosphere, Register, and
Voice*. Register is a use-defined variety of language described by co-occurring
situational features; it answers a different governing question from attributed
attitude and felt quality, and register, genre, style, dialect, and idiolect
form their own candidate family. This brief keeps one bounded family — stance
and attributed affect — and carries register, genre, and style only as context
factors. That deferred half is closed by the separate
[register, style, and genre brief](register-style-genre-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md).

## Bounded thesis

Proceed only as an attributed-stance and felt-quality entry. Semantic content,
interpretation, perceptual appearance, signal-level prosody, situational
language variation, and organizational dissent channels retain their existing
or candidate owners.

The thesis is falsified if stance, tone, narrative voice, felt mood, and
atmosphere can be answered completely by semantic meaning plus interpretation
plus appearance without losing the attribution locus, the duration and
object-directedness of the affective state, or the spatial extension of an
ambient quality; or if the five homonym clusters cannot be excluded by name
without emptying the family.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `stance` | Which attitude toward the proposition, the addressee, or the subject is explicitly encoded by attitude, engagement, and graduation resources? |
| `tone` | Which attitude is attributed to the producer or the work toward its subject and audience, on which cited textual or perceptible evidence? |
| `narrative-voice` | Who speaks or narrates, at which level, with which relation to the story, as against who perceives? |
| `felt-mood` | Which diffuse, low-intensity, weakly object-directed affective state is induced in or ascribed to the receiver? |
| `atmosphere` | Which affective quality is experienced as spatially extended over a scene, place, or setting? |

## Candidate contract

```text
stance-tone-atmosphere-use
  := expression, work, utterance, performance, or place identity
   x producer, performer, or designer position
   x receiver, audience, and reception conditions
   x medium, modality, genre, and register context
   x textual, prosodic, visual, or spatial evidence cited
   x encoded stance resources of attitude, engagement, and graduation
   x attributed tone and its target of subject, audience, or self
   x induced or ascribed mood, its duration, intensity, and object-directedness
   x atmospheric quality, its spatial extent, and its onset conditions
   x attribution locus of producer, work, receiver, or convention
   x disagreement, alternative readings, cultural variation, and revision
```

## Counterevidence and limits

- Every title word is severely polysemous. Tone is also phonological lexical
  tone, musical timbre, and pictorial value; mood is also grammatical mood and
  Genettian narrative mood; voice is also grammatical diathesis, phonatory
  voice, and organizational dissent; atmosphere is also the meteorological
  envelope and a pressure unit. Homonym exclusion is the largest admission risk.
- Tone attribution is contested: the same textual evidence supports competing
  attributions, and cultural convention changes which reading is available.
- Atmosphere as a quasi-objective category is a live aesthetic proposal, not a
  measured property; it must not become a hidden perception owner.
- The emotion/mood boundary has evidence-based criteria but no single accepted
  threshold, so duration and object-directedness stay as recorded factors.
- Stance resources are language-specific; an appraisal analysis of one language
  does not transfer without restatement.

## Admission gates

1. Exclude grammatical mood, grammatical voice, phonological tone, musical and
   pictorial tone, meteorological atmosphere, and organizational voice by name.
2. Keep encoded stance separate from attributed tone.
3. Keep tone separate from felt mood by attribution locus, not by intensity.
4. Keep felt mood separate from emotion by duration, intensity, and
   object-directedness, and record which criterion was used.
5. Keep atmosphere spatially extended and distinct from a subject's state.
6. Require cited evidence and a stated attribution basis for every claim.
7. Retain competing readings, cultural variation, and revision.
8. Route semantic content, interpretation, signal-level prosody, appearance,
   scoring, and situational variation to their existing owners.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.** The stance and
atmosphere distinctions are supported, but the homonym load and the boundary
against the register family must be resolved before any canonical admission is
considered.
