# Global Philosophy Counter-Sample Literature Review

Status: Complete; supports the staged blueprint, not global completeness

## Phase 1 — claims requiring support

### Claim 1

PFC-01 should be extended through several regional and transregional campaigns
rather than one residual “non-Western” list.

- Confirming evidence: field surveys and historiography showing multiple,
  internally diverse philosophical traditions and transmission networks.
- Skeptical pushback: dividing work into lanes can reify modern geographic,
  national, religious, or civilizational boundaries.

### Claim 2

Comparison can use bridge concepts and governing questions while retaining
untranslatable terms, internal disagreement, direction, and mapping loss.

- Confirming evidence: comparative-philosophy and translation methodology.
- Skeptical pushback: even bridge concepts may impose English-language or
  Factorium categories on traditions whose philosophical forms differ.

### Claim 3

Arabic-language, Islamic, Persianate, Indian, Buddhist, Chinese, Russian,
African, Indigenous, and other labels must remain distinct and contextual
rather than interchangeable demographic categories.

- Confirming evidence: specialist reference works that explicitly define each
  field's language, geography, religion, period, and transmission boundaries.
- Skeptical pushback: a proliferating label system can still conceal mixed,
  diasporic, oral, practice-based, and contested identities.

### Claim 4

Factorium should admit a cross-tradition owner only when reusable structure
recurs across independently sourced packets; culturally specific concepts can
remain scoped views or routes.

- Confirming evidence: comparative method, conceptual history, and ontology
  mapping work that distinguish analogy from equivalence.
- Skeptical pushback: recurrence thresholds can privilege structures already
  legible to a universalizing schema and exclude singular but important ideas.

### Claim 5

Indigenous and community-held philosophies require stronger source-custody and
context gates than open extraction from a general encyclopedia.

- Confirming evidence: Indigenous research ethics and community authority
  frameworks.
- Skeptical pushback: a single generic custody rule cannot substitute for the
  protocols of a particular people or knowledge community.

## Phase 4 — evidence position and gaps

Claims with strong support: 5/5

Claims with weak or no support: none at blueprint level; individual lane
coverage and packet selection remain untested.

Contrary evidence to address: Zorrilla 2022 and Connell/Janssen-Lauret 2022
show that representative-name sampling can reproduce exclusions; Zhang 2010
shows that a shared label is insufficient evidence of conceptual identity.

HIGH RISK claims: Claim 1 and Claim 4

- Claim 1 — scope: call this an expandable sampling frame, never “all cultures”
  or world-philosophy completeness. Lanes are work partitions, not natural
  civilizational containers.
- Claim 4 — qualify: recurrence is only screening pressure. A singular,
  culturally specific structure may deserve a scoped view, while a recurring
  English gloss may still fail exact owner admission.

Overall evidence strength: strong for staging, terminology discipline,
translation-loss fields, and Indigenous custody gates; insufficient to freeze
the actual packets in any lane without lane-specific source work.
