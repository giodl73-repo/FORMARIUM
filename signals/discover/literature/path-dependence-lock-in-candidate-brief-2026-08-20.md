# Path Dependence, Lock-In, Switching Cost, Increasing Returns, Critical Juncture, and Reactive Sequence — Candidate Brief

## Decision question

Should Lexicon add historically conditioned mechanisms while rejecting generic
historical causation and emergence as senses?

## Bounded thesis

Yes, provisionally. The six senses require a specified sequence, mechanism,
alternatives, and evidence; they do not label every durable or emergent outcome
as path dependent.

## Proposed senses

| Sense ID | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| `path-dependence-mechanism` | Which earlier sequence changes later feasible paths through a specified reproducing mechanism? |
| `lock-in-state` | Which self-reinforcing configuration persists despite identified alternatives and switching conditions? |
| `switching-cost` | Which cost of changing from a current arrangement to an alternative is evidenced for whom and when? |
| `adoption-increasing-returns` | Which adoption feedback makes an option relatively more attractive as adoption accumulates? |
| `critical-juncture` | Which bounded choice interval has durable, mechanism-traced downstream consequences? |
| `reactive-sequence` | Which event sequence is linked by reactions that condition subsequent events? |

## Candidate contract

Record sequence, baseline, alternatives, actors, mechanism, feedback, costs,
scope, counterfactual, evidence, and uncertainty. A historical sequence alone
does not satisfy the contract.

## Existing-owner audit

`causal-reasoning` owns general causal effects and mechanisms; tipping/critical
transition owns nonlinear transition patterns; operational owners own current
constraints. Emergence and generic historical causation are routed, not senses.

## Admission gates

Require explicit counterfactuals, mechanism evidence, and reviews against
causal, emergence, and critical-transition overlap.

## Counterevidence and limits

The term “path dependence” is used inconsistently across fields; selection,
learning, or ordinary persistence may explain cases instead. No irreversible or
causal conclusion follows automatically.

## Disposition

Keep noncanonical; reject emergence and generic historical causation ownership.
