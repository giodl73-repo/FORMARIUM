# GPC-02 South Asian and Transregional Buddhist Literature Review

## Phase 1 — claims requiring support

1. The eighteen frozen position-spaces are a defensible first counter-sample,
   not exhaustive coverage. Specialist surveys can confirm breadth; canon and
   exclusion studies can show whose work the sample still misses.
2. Source-local structures pressure PFC-01's epistemic, normative, agency,
   mind, meaning, metaphysical, and social gap families. School-specific
   studies can confirm this; the Western vocabulary of those families is the
   principal skeptical objection.
3. No one factoring represents “Indian philosophy” or “Buddhism.” Histories of
   disagreement and transmission can confirm plurality; school packets may
   nevertheless harden porous boundaries.
4. English glosses are directional and lossy. Translation and philosophy-of-
   language work can confirm that terms change by use and dispute; leaving
   terms untranslated can itself exoticize or conceal disagreement.
5. Immutable local factoring before alignment is a defensible comparative
   method. Bridge-concept methods support it; every factoring remains an
   editorial reconstruction rather than neutral access to a tradition.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Factorium proceeds from PFC-01's Western-derived gap families
directly into content design without testing whether South Asian and Buddhist
traditions reorganize knowledge, personhood, action, mind, meaning, reality,
and liberation around different pivots and dependencies.
