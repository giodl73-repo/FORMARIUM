# Philosopher-to-Factorium Comparison Literature Review

Status: Complete; suitable for a bounded comparison, not a canon claim

## Phase 1 — claims requiring support

### Claim 1

A philosopher's work can be compared with Factorium by extracting governing
questions, reusable concepts, and decisive distinctions without making the
philosopher a canonical headword.

- Confirming evidence: methodological precedent in history of philosophy,
  conceptual analysis, and comparative philosophy.
- Skeptical pushback: a system's concepts may be historically situated and
  mutually dependent, so atomizing them can erase the argument.

### Claim 2

An initial longitudinal sample from Socrates through Rawls can reveal whether
Factorium's present structural vocabulary reaches ethics, epistemology,
metaphysics, agency, language, politics, and justice.

- Confirming evidence: primary texts plus scholarly syntheses that identify
  stable questions and distinctions.
- Skeptical pushback: a “great philosophers” sample is canon-dependent and
  cannot represent philosophy generally.

### Claim 3

Coverage must distinguish an existing concept owner from a missing search
route, a partially represented distinction, and a true reusable concept gap.

- Confirming evidence: methodological work on concept comparison, translation,
  and historically responsible reconstruction.
- Skeptical pushback: mapping heterogeneous vocabularies into one schema may
  manufacture equivalence.

### Claim 4

Proper names, named doctrines, schools, and work titles should remain source
provenance or examples unless their underlying structure recurs independently.

- Confirming evidence: historiographical and comparative-method arguments
  against canon-as-taxonomy and decontextualized label matching.
- Skeptical pushback: removing named formulations may discard philosophically
  important structures that have no neutral paraphrase.
