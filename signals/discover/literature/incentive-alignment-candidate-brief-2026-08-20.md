# Incentive, Alignment, Externality, Moral Hazard, and Adverse Selection

## Decision question

Does Lexicon need an owner for the strategic relation in which one party makes a
consequence contingent on an observable in order to change another party's
choice, together with the information-asymmetry and incidence conditions under
which such relations succeed, fail, or spill outside the decision boundary?

## Bounded thesis

This family advances two conjoined contracts. They are stated separately because
they are separately falsifiable, and the family should be admitted only if both
survive.

**Contract A, the designed contingency relation.** `incentive` and
`incentive-compatibility` own the relation in which one party deliberately makes
a consequence contingent on a named observable in order to change another
party's choice, and the property claim that a stated mechanism induces the
intended behavior under a stated equilibrium concept. The family disclaims the
magnitude, the target and measure, the rule or policy instrument, the obligation
or requirement statement, and the delegation structure.

*Falsification test for Contract A:* the contract fails if cost and value,
objective and indicator, obligation, requirement statement, policy instrument,
and delegation together express every designed-contingency case without losing
the conditioning observable, the timing, or the specific choice intended to
change.

**Contract B, the information-asymmetry and incidence conditions.**
`alignment`, `externality`, `moral-hazard`, and `adverse-selection` own the
conditions under which pursued ends diverge, effects fall on non-parties, care
or effort changes after an agreement, and pool composition shifts before one.

*Falsification test for Contract B:* the contract fails if preference, outcome,
causal attribution, risk, sampling, and compliance together express every
divergence, spillover, post-agreement behavior change, and pre-agreement
selection case without losing asymmetry timing, effect sign and non-party
incidence, ends-attribution basis, or proxy divergence.

At least five canonical owners hold parts of the surrounding territory: cost,
price, value, utility, and return owns payoff magnitude and utility; choice owns
preference, criteria, and decision rules; objective and control owns objective,
indicator, and threshold; governance owns `obligation` as a binding duty, while
requirement and specification statements belong to the requirement owner; and
organization owns delegation and accountability.

`incentive` is assigned to this strategic and economic family only. The internal
conative side, what an agent wants or is settled on, stays with the motive,
desire, intention, and rationale candidate. Motivation crowding is the
documented interaction between the two, and is a factor, not evidence that the
senses are the same.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `incentive` | Which contingent consequence, offered by whom to whom, conditional on which observable, is intended to change which choice? |
| `incentive-compatibility` | Under which mechanism and equilibrium concept is it demonstrated that each agent's own payoff-maximizing behavior coincides with the designer's intended behavior? |
| `alignment` | Between which principal's ends, attributed on what basis, and which agent's pursued objective is divergence assessed, over what scope, horizon, and distribution of situations? |
| `externality` | Which uncompensated effect of a decision, beneficial or harmful, falls on a party outside the decision or transaction boundary, and is it Pareto-relevant? |
| `moral-hazard` | Which action, effort, or care changes after an agreement because the actor no longer bears the full consequence, whether through unobservability or through a changed marginal price? |
| `adverse-selection` | Which pre-agreement private information about type causes pool composition to shift under a common term or price? |

## Candidate contract

```text
incentive-alignment-use
  := principal, designer, or regulator
   x agent, respondent, or optimizing system
   x decision or behavior intended to change
   x contingent consequence, magnitude basis, timing, and delivery
   x observable, measure, proxy, and verification cost
   x mechanism, equilibrium concept, and demonstration status
   x information asymmetry type, timing, and direction
   x marginal price, incidence of consequence, and exposure change
   x action space, effort dimensions, and multitask trade-offs
   x participation, self-selection, screening, and signaling
   x decision boundary, affected non-parties, effect sign, and compensation
     status
   x principal ends, ends-attribution basis, agent pursued objective, horizon,
     distribution shift, and side effects
   x internalization, monitoring, contract, rule, or mechanism instrument
   x observed response, gaming, crowding-out, and revision
```

## Counterevidence and limits

- an incentive that changes no behavior;
- an incentive that changes the measured behavior and worsens the intended
  outcome;
- a mechanism whose incentive compatibility holds under one equilibrium concept
  and fails under another, or is asserted without demonstration;
- misalignment with no incentive present, arising from ambiguity of ends;
- alignment achieved without any incentive, by shared ends or by obligation;
- an alignment claim whose principal ends cannot be attributed to any identified
  party on a stated basis;
- a positive externality, such as an uncompensated benefit to a non-party, which
  is as much an externality as an uncompensated harm;
- an externality that is real and not Pareto-relevant, so no intervention is
  warranted;
- a cost borne by a party inside the transaction, which is a price and not an
  externality;
- moral hazard without any moral fault, as a rational response to a changed
  marginal price, even where the action is fully observable;
- moral hazard driven by unobservability of effort where the marginal price is
  unchanged;
- adverse selection with no hidden action after the agreement;
- moral hazard with no hidden type before the agreement;
- selection arising from how a sample was drawn, which belongs to sampling and
  not to adverse selection;
- an incentive that crowds out an existing motive;
- unit, scale, or dimension alignment in a measurement, which is not this
  family's sense of alignment at all.

## Admission gates

1. Every `incentive` row names the designer, the target agent, the observable
   the consequence is conditioned on, the timing, the magnitude basis owned
   elsewhere, and the specific choice intended to change. If any of these six is
   absent, the row belongs to its existing owner.
2. Keep `incentive` separate from price, reward, compensation, obligation,
   requirement statement, governance control, policy instrument, and delegation.
3. Keep `incentive` separate from `incentive-compatibility`; the latter names a
   mechanism, an equilibrium concept, and the status of its demonstration.
4. Assign the internal conative reading of "what motivates" to the motive
   candidate; this family owns only the designed external contingency.
5. Scope `alignment` to ends congruence between a principal and an agent, and
   require an attribution basis for the principal's ends and for the agent's
   pursued objective, with artificial-system alignment as one domain instance
   rather than a topic page.
6. Bound `alignment` explicitly against unit, scale, dimension, and time-base
   alignment, which remain with the quantity owners.
7. Preserve both moral-hazard readings: changed behavior under unobservability,
   and changed behavior under a changed marginal price or incidence without any
   hiddenness. State that neither imputes fault.
8. Keep the moral hazard and adverse selection split on timing and on what
   drives them: post-agreement change in action or care, whether from
   unobservability or shifted incidence, versus private type before the
   agreement.
9. Treat `externality` as sign-neutral: record beneficial and harmful effects
   alike, with the effect sign, the declared decision boundary, the affected
   non-party, and the Pareto-relevance test on every row.
10. Do not claim agency cost as an amount; cite it as a factor and defer the
    magnitude.
11. Stop before compensation-scheme and pay-for-performance catalogs, carbon
    pricing and environmental policy design, insurance product design, contract
    theory proofs and equilibrium derivations, nudge catalogs, corporate
    governance codes, and artificial-intelligence safety technique catalogs.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.** Source quality
is the strongest of the current batch and ownership is the weakest. Admission
requires that both contracts survive a fixed-point role review independently:
the designed-contingency relation against cost, choice, control, governance,
requirement, policy, and organization, and the asymmetry-and-incidence
conditions against outcome, causal attribution, risk, and sampling. This brief
is not financial, legal, or policy advice.
