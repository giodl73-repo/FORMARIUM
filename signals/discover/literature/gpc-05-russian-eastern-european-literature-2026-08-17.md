# GPC-05 Russian and Eastern European Literature Review

## Phase 1 — claims

1. Six packets can expose structural counterexamples but cannot represent
   Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Czech, Orthodox, Soviet, émigré, or Eastern
   European philosophy. Histories establish breadth; the low floor and major
   omissions directly defeat completeness.
2. Community/person, all-unity/freedom, polyphonic voice, ideality/activity,
   affinity/vocation, intentional object, and dissident responsibility are
   distinct pivots. Surveys support comparison; national-character readings
   and shared translated glosses threaten it.
3. Literary, theological, phenomenological, logical, Marxist, and dissident
   forms produce different kinds of warrant. Method histories confirm this;
   treating each as doctrine propositions loses genre and practice.
4. Imperial rule, Soviet orthodoxy and repression, exile, partition,
   occupation, and dissent constrain what was written and institutionally
   recognized. Histories confirm the constraints; a clean nation-by-nation
   narrative can reproduce political custody claims.
5. Community, freedom, nonviolence, revolution, nation, and living in truth
   cannot become present guidance. Sources support historical analysis;
   normative or geopolitical uptake requires qualified review.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Factorium maps a “Russian and Eastern European” list to
generic community, freedom, relation, activity, identity, and truth owners,
making Russian philosophy the default, literary and dissident forms into
propositions, and imperial or Soviet custody invisible.
