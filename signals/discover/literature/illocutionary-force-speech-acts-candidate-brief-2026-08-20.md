# Illocutionary Force and Speech Acts — Candidate Brief

## Decision question

Should Lexicon add a bounded illocutionary-force layer distinct from meaning,
dialogue transport, intention, and institutional outcome?

## Bounded thesis

Yes, provisionally. The six broad force families answer what communicative act
is undertaken, while nearby entries answer what content means, what message was
exchanged, or what outcome followed.

## Proposed senses

| Sense ID | Governing question |
| --- | --- |
| `illocutionary-assertive` | Is the speaker undertaking an assertive presentation of content? |
| `illocutionary-question` | Which information is the speaker seeking to have the addressee supply as an answer? |
| `illocutionary-directive` | Which action other than supplying an answer is the speaker attempting to get the addressee to perform? |
| `illocutionary-commissive` | Is the speaker committing themselves to a future course? |
| `illocutionary-expressive` | Is the speaker expressing an evaluative or affective stance toward a situation? |
| `illocutionary-declarative` | Does an authorized utterance purport to alter an institutional status? |

## Candidate contract

Record utterance, participants, content, context, force attribution, authority,
uptake, and alternatives. Point, felicity conditions, and dialogue move are
evidence-bearing factors only.

## Existing-owner audit

`meaning-reference-interpretation-use` owns meaning; interaction owns
request/response transport; motive/intention owns private attitudes; governance
and institutional concepts own status authority. None owns force itself.

## Source matrix

| Source | Contribution | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SEP, Speech Acts](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/speech-acts/) | Supports force, indirectness, uptake, and felicity distinctions. | It does not make Searle's taxonomy exhaustive across languages. |
| [ISO 24617-2 record](https://www.iso.org/standard/76443.html) | Provides a dialogue-act annotation comparison point. | The standard is an annotation scheme, not a universal theory of speech acts. |

## Admission gates

Require examples of indirect force and authorized/unauthorized declarations,
an explicit question/directive selection test, and review that factors have not
become senses. Preserve taxonomies that treat questions as a directive subtype
without assigning the same act to both senses in one selected view.

## Counterevidence and limits

Speech-act taxonomies and felicity conditions are contested and language
dependent. The candidate does not determine sincerity, legal effect, or a
speaker's intention.

## Disposition

Keep as a noncanonical force candidate; retain point, felicity, and dialogue
move as factors.
