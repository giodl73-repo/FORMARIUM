# GPC-03 Chinese, Korean, and Japanese Literature Review

## Phase 1 — claims

1. Fourteen packets satisfy the blueprint's lane floors without constituting
   an East Asian canon. Surveys confirm breadth; feminist, colonial, minority,
   and modern political critique challenge the sample.
2. Ritual, cultivation, heart-mind, pattern, vital force, models, standards,
   ways, and relational selfhood pressure current Factorium owners. Specialist
   analyses confirm the structures; familiar English categories may distort.
3. Chinese transmission into Korea and Japan produced local arguments rather
   than copies. Reception histories confirm adaptation; nation-bounded packets
   can still hide transregional continuity.
4. Shared graphs and English glosses do not establish equivalence. Translation
   studies and divergent uses confirm directional loss; retained source terms
   can still appear falsely precise.
5. Political appropriation belongs in the mapping boundary. Histories of
   Legalism, Confucian hierarchy, Kokugaku, and Kyoto School nationalism
   confirm risk; no tradition should be reduced to its appropriation.
