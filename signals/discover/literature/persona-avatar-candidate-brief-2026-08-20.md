# Persona, Self-Presentation, Mask, Disguise, and Avatar

## Decision question

Does Lexicon need one owner for the relation between an underlying agent, a
deliberately constructed figure that the agent projects to an audience, and the
declared basis on which the figure's conduct is attributed to the agent?

## Scope decision

The originally proposed family was *Manifestation, Embodiment, Persona,
Disguise, and Avatar*. Those terms answer two unrelated questions.
Manifestation and embodiment ask how something abstract, latent, or general
becomes concrete, present, or bodily; persona, disguise, and avatar ask how an
agent presents a controlled identity through a mediating figure. This brief
keeps only the second question.

Manifestation is deliberately absent from the title and from the senses. It is
a term of art in bibliographic conceptual models, a disposition-display term, a
clinical sign term, and a religious term; embodiment is contested
phenomenology whose neighbouring senses are already claimed by classification
and by the perception candidate. That deferred half is closed by the separate
[type, token, instantiation, and realization
brief](instantiation-realization-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md).

## Bounded thesis

Proceed only as a projected-identity entry. Entity identity and identifiers,
organizational role and accountability, anonymity and pseudonymity, rendered
representation, and perceptual appearance retain their existing or candidate
owners.

The thesis is falsified if persona, mask, disguise, and avatar can be recorded
completely as aliases of one entity identity without losing the audience, the
purpose, the disclosure status, the controlled and concealed attributes, or the
declared attribution basis; or if the persona, disguise, and avatar distinctions
collapse into anonymity and pseudonymity as already owned.

## Proposed senses

| Sense | Governing question |
|---|---|
| `self-presentation` | Which impression is given, and given off, to which audience in which situated performance? |
| `persona` | Which constructed public identity is adopted, by whom, for which audience and purpose, and is it disclosed? |
| `design-persona` | Which fictional archetypal user, grounded in which research data, stands in for which real segment during design? |
| `mask` | Which visible covering or figure enables assumption of another identity or state, within which convention? |
| `disguise` | Which identity attribute is deliberately concealed or falsified, from whom, with what deceptive intent? |
| `avatar` | Which digital figure represents and is controlled by which user in which mediated environment? |
| `conduct-attribution-basis` | Under which declared basis is conduct by the figure attributed to an underlying agent, and is that basis disclosed? |

## Candidate contract

```text
persona-presentation-avatar-use
  := underlying agent or originator identity
   x figure, persona, mask, or avatar identity
   x medium, platform, environment, and convention
   x audience, co-participants, setting, and front or back region
   x purpose of performance, ritual, protection, play, research proxy, or deception
   x disclosure status of open, pseudonymous, anonymous, or deceptive
   x controlled attributes of name, appearance, voice, body, and capabilities
   x concealed or falsified attributes and concealment intent
   x degree of control, coupling, and real-time responsiveness
   x behavioural and perceptual effects on the agent and on observers
   x declared attribution basis and its disclosure to the audience
   x duration, reversibility, and multiplicity of figures per agent
```

## Existing-owner audit

- [Identity, Namespace, Naming, Classification, and Versioning](../../../tables/entries/identity-naming-classification-versioning.md)
  owns entity identity, identifier, name, and alias. An alias is a reference
  token for the same entity; a persona is a presented identity with its own
  attributes, audience, and purpose. This distinction is the primary collision
  and must be stated in the draft.
- [Organization, Position, Role, Competency, Responsibility, Authority, and
  Accountability](../../../tables/entries/organization-role-authority.md)
  owns organizational role, answerability, and accountability; who answers for
  a figure's conduct routes there. This family records only the declared basis
  on which conduct is attributed, and whether that basis was disclosed.
- [Model, Representation, and Simulation](../../../tables/entries/model-representation-simulation.md)
  owns representation; an avatar considered as a rendered representation routes
  there, while the agent-figure-audience relation stays here.
- [Privacy, Confidentiality, Secrecy, Anonymity, and
  Disclosure](../candidates/privacy-confidentiality-secrecy-anonymity-disclosure.md)
  owns anonymity, pseudonymity, identifiability, and linkability, and therefore
  owns whether an agent-to-figure attribution can technically be established and
  the re-identification risk of establishing it. This family owns only
  deliberate projection of a false or constructed identity and the declared
  attribution basis.
- [Perception, Sensation, Appearance, Illusion, and
  Hallucination](../candidates/perception-sensation-appearance-illusion-hallucination.md)
  owns appearance and illusion; perceptual mismatch is not re-owned here.

## Counterevidence and limits

- Persona, mask, and avatar are severely polysemous: a psychic structure, a
  dramaturgical front, a rhetorical construct, a design artifact, a theatrical
  object, a ritual instrument, a digital figure, and a religious descent.
- The digital avatar and the religious avatara share an etymology but not a
  concept; flattening them misrepresents religious traditions.
- Design personas without research grounding are criticized as fiction, so the
  evidence basis of a design persona is a required factor rather than optional.
- A figure is not inert: experimental evidence shows a chosen representation
  can change the represented user's own behaviour.
- The concealment against falsification boundary in deception ethics is
  disputed, so intent evidence must be recorded rather than inferred.
- Persona use is not by itself deceptive; open and conventional personas are
  common, and this entry must not treat constructed identity as wrongdoing.
- This brief does not assess platform policy, legal identity requirements, or
  the legitimacy of any particular concealment.

## Admission gates

1. Distinguish a persona from an alias by attributes, audience, and purpose.
2. Distinguish a persona from an organizational role by projection against
   institutional assignment.
3. Require deceptive intent and concealed or falsified attributes for
   `disguise`; keep non-deceptive figures out of that sense.
4. Do not re-own anonymity, pseudonymity, identifiability, linkability, or
   re-identification risk; technical establishability of an attribution routes
   to the privacy candidate.
5. Keep the avatar against autonomous-agent boundary explicit through real-time
   human control.
6. Exclude the religious avatara, the Jungian archetype outside its cited
   psychological sense, grammatical person, and legal person by name.
7. Require an evidence basis for every design persona.
8. Record the declared attribution basis and whether it was disclosed; route
   answerability for the conduct to the organization and accountability entry.
9. Route rendering, representation, appearance, and identifier semantics to
   their existing owners.

## Disposition

**PROCEED TO CANDIDATE ENTRY DRAFT; DO NOT ADMIT CANONICALLY.** The
agent-to-figure relation is unclaimed and supported, but the alias boundary and
the privacy candidate boundary require review before canonical admission. The
manifestation and embodiment half of the original proposal is deferred.
