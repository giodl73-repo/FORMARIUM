# Formography Foundation Risk Register

## Inertia risk

Current workaround: describe the work through factorization, concept maps,
graphs, mappings, and reference architecture without naming a new field.

Switching cost: readers and reviewers must learn `form`, `formograph`, and
Formography while distinguishing them from established neighboring terms.

Inertia threat: **HIGH**.

Inertia loses only if the field contract materially simplifies explanation and
produces a repeatable artifact that existing methods omit.

| Dimension | Risk | Severity | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research integrity | Exact STS use is overlooked or minimized | HIGH | MEDIUM | cite and contrast it in every field-defining paper |
| Novelty | Established primitives are bundled and mislabeled as a new field | HIGH | HIGH | attach novelty only to the surviving contract and comparative outputs |
| Method | Core objects cannot be coded consistently | HIGH | MEDIUM | frozen manual, independent coding, retained disagreements |
| Adoption | Name sounds like branding rather than a needed method | MEDIUM | HIGH | terminology test against plain existing-language explanation |
| Representation | Topic Maps or property graphs reproduce everything | HIGH | MEDIUM | comparative encoding and reduction tests |
| Domain | `form` changes meaning silently across fields | HIGH | MEDIUM | cross-domain cases and explicit extension rules |
| Product | Formarium identity drives the field definition | MEDIUM | MEDIUM | specify multiple compliant artifact forms before publication architecture |
| Claims | Local Factorium corpus is reported as external validation | HIGH | MEDIUM | keep local evidence and independent evidence separate |

Overall risk: **HIGH but researchable**.
