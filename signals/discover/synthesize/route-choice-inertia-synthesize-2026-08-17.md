## Agreement and conflict map

### Agreement

1. The hypothesis, design review, roles review, and result all agree that only
   lexical reach is mechanical; route choice, trust, memory, fun, and density
   remain authored hypotheses.
2. The inertia analysis, SUJ-01, and SUJ-02 agree that incumbent references
   retain orientation and domain-authority advantages.
3. SUJ-01, SUJ-02, and the practitioner review agree that Factorium's distinct
   value begins when a problem needs distinctions, composition, a stopping
   rule, or an outward handoff.
4. The result and product review agree that term-blind tasks need two prominent
   entrances: Search for a direct structural landing and Compose for a
   multi-owner question.

### Conflicts

1. The frozen expectation treated “long or short light” as a miss, while the
   exact trace places Periodic and Wave Quantity fifth. Mechanical custody wins;
   the authored classification was corrected before publication.
2. SUJ-01 includes several replacement-shaped definition tasks, while SUJ-02's
   inertia model predicts only supplementation or structural switching. They
   diverge because SUJ-02 deliberately begins with problem-shaped prompts, not
   canonical term lookups.
3. The strong 24/25 lexical reach could suggest a broad product claim, but the
   HIGH-inertia signal and absence of readers forbid that inference.

### Isolated findings

1. Tables A–Z receives no authored first-entrance assignment. This is useful
   navigation guidance but remains uncorroborated by reader behavior.
2. The green-dot status prompt is the sole vocabulary-injection miss. One
   dependent synthetic case is insufficient to admit a search repair.
3. The view-profile distribution is task-fit authorship, not evidence of
   preference or readability.

## Cross-signal insights

1. Factorium's likely differentiator is not owning the first fact; it is
   preserving the route from an ordinary question through concepts to an
   explicit stop and authoritative handoff. This follows from SUJ-01's bridge
   pattern, SUJ-02's supplement pattern, and the inertia analysis.
2. Search and Compose express different task contracts. Search is the likely
   direct entrance for 14 authored routes; Compose is the likely entrance for
   eight routes whose value depends on multiple owners. They should not be
   collapsed into one undifferentiated search promise.
3. Tables A–Z remains important after a term or family is recognized, but the
   term-blind portfolio provides no reason to make it the primary problem-led
   entrance.
4. Trust is rehearsed as visible boundaries, sources, evidence status, and
   unresolved questions—not an appearance of completeness. That is consistent
   with Factorium's reference custody and outward-handoff design.
5. Situation-shaped memory cues may matter more than headwords: “green but not
   usable,” “percent of what,” or “passed but not decided” encode the route's
   reusable structure. This is a testable reader hypothesis, not a result.

## Decision implication

**Answer: PROCEED. Confidence: 82/100.**

Proceed to an external-reader-ready, term-blind observation design centered on
Search, Compose, stopping, and handoff. Do not change content, ranking, aliases,
or navigation from SUJ-02 alone.

The case for proceeding is the frozen 24/25 family-level lexical reach plus a
coherent structural value proposition across two campaigns. The case for
pausing is HIGH incumbent inertia and zero observed reader behavior. A pivot
would be warranted only if real readers cannot recognize when to move from
ordinary orientation into Factorium's structural route.

Missing signals that could change the decision are observed term-blind route
choice, task completion with outward handoff, comparative use against current
reference chains, and accessibility/readability evidence across density views.
