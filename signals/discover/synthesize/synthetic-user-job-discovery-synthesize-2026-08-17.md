## Agreement and conflict map

### Agreement

- The hypothesis, design review, roles review, and execution agree that the
  plausible product is not a universal dictionary replacement.
- All four signals agree that exact ownership, visible losses, and stopping
  boundaries—not more prose—create the distinctive mechanism.
- Design and role reviews agree that persona breadth cannot establish
  demographic, behavioral, accessibility, or market conclusions.

### Conflict

- The product hypothesis expects a distinct reason to visit, while four of
  five lookup controls are ordinary replacement jobs and one is better served
  elsewhere. The conflict resolves by task: Factorium's distinct value begins
  after the one-term lookup, not before it.
- Twenty routes satisfy the authored post-definition mechanism, but scenario
  selection was designed around Factorium-relevant concepts. This supports
  technical capability, not natural demand frequency.

### Isolated signal

- Exact `alias` lookup is poor for a one-line synonym task. It is a retained
  null and useful positioning boundary, but one synthetic query does not earn
  a search repair.

## Cross-signal insights

### 1. Factorium is a second reference move

Dictionary and thesaurus journeys most often become bridges: nine of twelve
such starts continue into structured comparison, while only one becomes a
new-reason classification, one remains replacement, and one is not served.

Implication: position Factorium after “What does this word mean?”—at “Which
meaning applies, how does it relate, what conditions matter, and where must I
stop?”

### 2. Purpose changes the plausible job

All fifteen practical-purpose journeys classify as bridge or new reason. The
ten curiosity journeys are mixed: four replacement, four bridge, one new
reason, and one not served.

Implication: the primary promise should be purposeful distinction and
composition. Fun browsing is valuable as discovery and learning, but should
remain lighter and should not carry the core value claim.

### 3. The new reason begins from a problem, not a headword

Five of the six new-reason routes begin where the declared alternative is web
search or workplace documentation. Their outputs are dependency diagnosis,
mixture scaling, capacity analysis, access-decision trace, or decision-rule
composition—not a better definition.

Implication: preserve the dictionary/thesaurus Tables entrance, but pair it
with an equally clear “work through a question” entrance.

### 4. Existing content already supports the mechanism

All 25 selected routes resolve in `sim-42`, no route requires a missing owner,
and the campaign admits no content repair. The isolated available/unusable
friction was already known and this intentionally selected portfolio does not
constitute independent recurrence.

Implication: test language and route choice with real readers before expanding
content or adding another simulation edition.

## Decision implication

**Answer:** PROCEED

**Confidence:** 72/100 for the positioning hypothesis; no confidence estimate
is assigned to adoption or reader behavior.

**Rationale:** Together, the signals support a clear technical/product
mechanism: Factorium complements ordinary lexical references and adds a
problem-led structural job. They do not establish that people recognize,
prefer, or return for that job.

**Case for proceeding:** The current reference resolves all 25 frozen routes
and exposes post-definition structure in 20.

**Case for pausing:** Synthetic personas cannot validate demand, usability,
accessibility, or messaging comprehension.

**Case for pivoting:** Do not market Factorium as a replacement dictionary;
frame it as the next step after naming and synonym lookup.

**Recommendation:** Proceed by carrying this two-entrance positioning into
real R4E prompts and observation, while pausing new content, aliases, or UI
features until actual route-choice evidence exists.

## Signal gaps

- External reader observation: whether people naturally recognize the
  post-definition job and choose the appropriate entrance.
- Inertia/alternative research: how people currently combine dictionaries,
  search, handbooks, documentation, and ad hoc notes for these questions.
- Accessibility evidence: whether the deeper routes remain usable under real
  reading and assistive-technology conditions.
