# Illocutionary Force and Speech Acts

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft

[Admission brief](../literature/illocutionary-force-speech-acts-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md)

## Orientation

This candidate identifies what a speaker is doing in uttering content under a
bounded conversational and institutional account. It distinguishes force from
literal meaning, transport interaction, attitude, and downstream effect.

## Sense table

| Sense ID | Governing question | Core |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `illocutionary-assertive` | Is the speaker undertaking an assertive presentation of content? | assertion force |
| `illocutionary-question` | Which information is the speaker seeking to have the addressee supply as an answer? | answer-seeking force |
| `illocutionary-directive` | Which action other than supplying an answer is the speaker attempting to get the addressee to perform? | non-answer action-seeking force |
| `illocutionary-commissive` | Is the speaker committing themselves to a future course? | self-commitment force |
| `illocutionary-expressive` | Is the speaker expressing an evaluative or affective stance toward a situation? | expressive force |
| `illocutionary-declarative` | Does an authorized utterance purport to alter an institutional status? | status-changing force |

## Root factorization

Factor speaker, addressee, content, point, strength, mode of achievement,
preparatory and sincerity conditions, authority, uptake, context, and evidence.
Illocutionary point, felicity conditions, and dialogue move are factors, not
senses.

## Decisive distinctions

The same sentence form may have different force. This entry records
answer-seeking acts as questions and action-seeking acts whose requested outcome
is not an answer as directives, while preserving sources that classify
questions as a directive subtype. A declarative needs relevant authority.
Commissive force differs from intention because it is communicatively
undertaken. Expressive force differs from having an emotion. Perlocutionary
effect and whether a dialogue system labels a move do not determine force by
themselves.

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Require an utterance or communicative act plus context. Route content,
reference, interpretation, transport request/response, consent, policy
authority, and emotional episodes to existing owners. Do not add illocutionary
point, felicity conditions, or dialogue move as senses.

## Selection procedure

Identify the attributed force using content, context, participants, and
conditions. Classify a sought answer as question force and another sought action
as directive force; record both source taxonomies where one treats questions as
a directive subtype. Record alternatives where indirectness is plausible. For
declarations, record the authority and institution rather than inferring effect.

## Reference Delta

Adds a force layer between meaning and interaction records, allowing their
contents and mechanics to remain distinct.

## Failure signs

Failure includes treating punctuation as decisive, equating successful uptake
with force, or classifying a declaration without authority and institution.
