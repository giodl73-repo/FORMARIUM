# Institution, Constitutive Norm, Regulative Norm, Social Object, Status Function, and Reified Relationship

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft

[Admission brief](../literature/institutional-concepts-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md)

## Orientation

This candidate supplies a plural, account-relative vocabulary for institutional
facts. It neither assumes a universal social ontology nor replaces organization,
role, governance, or relationship records.

## Sense table

| Sense ID | Governing question | Core |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `institutional-arrangement` | Which recurring, recognized arrangement organizes roles, practices, or positions in this account? | recognized arrangement |

## Root factorization

Factor community or authority, recognition, rule source, participants, status,
practice, record, scope, time, contestation, and evidence. Recognition,
collectivity, and reification are factors, not universal metaphysical facts.

## Decisive distinctions

Constitutive norms make a status possible within an account; regulative norms
govern a practice already intelligible there. A social object is not every
physical object used socially. Status function links a recognized status to a
role or power; it is not designed function. Reifying a relationship supports
records but does not prove it is an independent entity.

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Require a bounded account and evidence of recognition or representation. Route
organizations, positions, authority, obligation, policy, and relation mechanics
to existing owners. Do not assert that every society or database shares the
same ontology.

## Selection procedure

Identify the account, rule, practice, and participants. Test whether the rule
creates a status or regulates prior activity, and whether an objectification is
a modeling choice. Preserve disputes and alternative accounts.

## Reference Delta

Adds a controlled bridge between social-ontology analysis and existing
organizational/governance records.

## Failure signs

Failure signs are treating recognition as unanimous, assuming any relation is
a social object, or reading a data-model object as proof of ontological status.
