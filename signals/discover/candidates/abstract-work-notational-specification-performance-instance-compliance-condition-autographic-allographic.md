# Abstract Work, Notational Specification, Performance Instance, Compliance Condition, and Autographic/Allographic Distinction

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft

[Admission brief](../literature/abstract-work-notation-performance-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md)

## Orientation

This candidate maps a bounded work/notation/performance analysis useful for
cataloging and cultural-heritage description. It rejects ownership of aesthetic
object, aesthetic experience, and interpretation. Medium remains a factor
unless a future disjoint governing question is demonstrated.

## Root factorization

Factor account, work, notation, inscription, performance, agent, time, venue,
compliance condition, production history, version, medium, provenance, and
evidence. Medium is a factor because it does not yet pass a disjoint sense test.
Factors sharing a sense label are recording dimensions of the selected account,
not duplicate senses.

## Decisive distinctions

An abstract work is not a physical copy or performance. A notation specifies
features relevant to compliance; a performance instance realizes a work in
time. Compliance conditions are criteria, not the instance. Autographic versus
allographic is an account-relative distinction between production-history and
notation-compliance identity conditions. Interpretation and aesthetic value are
outside the boundary.

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Require a declared cataloging or ontological account. Route type/token,
instantiation, records, provenance, interpretation, and aesthetic experience to
their existing owners. Do not create a medium sense; record medium only where
evidence makes it relevant.

## Selection procedure

Identify whether the source refers to a work, notation, realized performance,
criterion, or identity-mode contrast. Record account and evidence; test whether
the claim is instead about a copy, medium, interpretation, or aesthetic
evaluation and route it.

## Reference Delta

Adds a controlled WEMI/Goodman-adjacent vocabulary without choosing one theory
of artworks or aesthetic objects.

## Failure signs

Failure includes treating every recording as a work, treating a score as a
performance, assuming notation suffices for every art form, or making medium an
unjustified disjoint sense.
