# Metaphor, Metonymy, Symbol, Allegory, and Allusion

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft; mechanism-owning, not interpretive

## Orientation

An expression is used figuratively when its contextual meaning contrasts with,
and is understood by reference to, its basic meaning. Metaphor structures
understanding of a primary subject through a secondary subject drawn from
another domain. Simile makes the comparison explicit with a comparative marker.
Metonymy substitutes one entity for an associated entity within a single
domain by contiguity, part-whole, or role. A symbol is an item that carries a
surplus of meaning beyond its literal reference by convention or context.
Allegory sustains a systematic second level of meaning across a whole work or
extended passage. Allusion indirectly evokes an identifiable prior text, work,
figure, or event.

This entry owns the mechanism of indirect signification. It does not own the
reading that a figure supports, the interpreter, the evidence for a reading, or
the resolution of interpretive disagreement.

The following homonyms are excluded by name: the notational symbol of a formula
symbol contract; the Peircean symbol as the conventional sign class contrasted
with icon and index; the psychological archetype; the national or corporate
emblem; and allegorical reading as an interpretive practice.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `literal-figurative-status` | Does the expression carry its more basic meaning, or a contextual meaning that contrasts with that basic meaning and is understood by reference to it? | identification precondition |
| `metaphor` | Which secondary subject structures understanding of which primary subject, and which correspondences are projected? | cross-domain mapping |
| `simile` | Which comparison is made explicit by a comparative marker, and how does the marking change the commitment? | marked comparison |
| `metonymy` | Which entity stands for which associated entity within one domain by contiguity, part-whole, or role? | within-domain substitution |
| `symbol` | Which item carries a surplus of meaning beyond its literal reference, and by whose convention or context? | surplus-signification item |
| `allegory` | Which sustained, systematic second level of meaning runs in parallel across a whole work or extended passage? | extended parallel level |
| `allusion` | Which identifiable prior text, work, figure, or event is indirectly evoked, and what does recognizing it add? | intertextual evocation |
| `figurative-inference` | Which correspondences does the figure project from source to target, which are blocked, and which conclusions are therefore prohibited? | projected and blocked correspondences |


## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphor vs. simile | both liken two subjects | predication with no comparative marker vs. explicit marked comparison with weaker commitment |
| Metaphor vs. metonymy | both substitute an expression | cross-domain mapping of structure vs. within-domain association by contiguity, part-whole, or role |
| Metaphor vs. analogy | both relate source to target | figurative use evaluated for aptness vs. a structural mapping used for inference, owned elsewhere |
| Metaphorical expression vs. conceptual mapping | both are called metaphor | one occurrence in discourse vs. a claimed systematic conceptual structure |
| Conventional vs. novel metaphor | both are metaphorical | lexicalized and possibly requiring no active mapping vs. deliberate and requiring interpretation |
| Symbol vs. metaphor | both carry non-literal meaning | a persisting item with open and often unstated meaning vs. a bounded expression with specifiable correspondences |
| Symbol vs. conventional sign | both signify | an item with contextual or cultural surplus vs. a sign class defined by convention against icon and index |
| Symbol vs. notational symbol | both use the word symbol | figurative surplus vs. a declared token in a symbol contract |
| Allegory vs. symbol | both add a second level | sustained systematic decodable correspondence across a whole vs. a single item with non-exhaustible meaning |
| Allegory vs. metaphor | both project one domain onto another | extended across a work with narrative continuity vs. local to an expression |
| Allegory as mode vs. allegorical reading | both are called allegory | a construction in the work vs. an interpretive practice applied to it |
| Allusion vs. quotation | both invoke a prior work | indirect evocation requiring recognition vs. marked verbatim reproduction |
| Allusion vs. intertextuality | both relate works | an attributable, recognizable, purposive reference vs. the general condition of textual relatedness |
| Motif vs. symbol | both persist across a work | recurrence is the criterion vs. surplus signification is the criterion |
| Figure vs. equivalence | both join two domains | a signification mechanism vs. a claim that the domains are the same under a criterion |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

- The expression, passage, image, or work is identified before a figure is
  claimed, together with its language, medium, period, and culture.
- Figurative status is established against a stated basic meaning rather than
  asserted.
- Every metaphor claim records both mapped and blocked correspondences.
- Every allusion claim records the referenced work, the identification
  evidence, and the audience that can recognize it.
- Conventionality, deliberateness, and lexicalization are factors, not senses.
- `analogy` retains the similarity candidate as its owner; `motif` retains the
  pattern candidate as its owner.
- Semantic meaning, reference, ambiguity, interpretation, and translation
  retain their existing owner.
- Identifying a figure licenses no equivalence, no causal claim, and no shared
  structure between the joined domains.

This entry stops before literary schools, periods, national canons, named
works, named authors, interpretive methods, rhetorical figure catalogues,
aesthetic quality judgments, and the correctness of any particular reading.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify the expression, passage, image, or work and its language, medium,
   genre, period, and culture.
2. Establish figurative status by comparing basic meaning with contextual
   meaning, and record the contrast.
3. Exclude the notational, sign-theoretic, archetypal, and emblematic senses of
   symbol by name before proceeding.
4. Select one mechanism: metaphor, simile, metonymy, symbol, allegory, or
   allusion.
5. For metaphor, name the primary and secondary subjects and list projected and
   blocked correspondences.
6. For metonymy, name the single domain and the contiguity, part-whole, or role
   relation.
7. For symbol, name the convention or context that supplies the surplus and the
   community that holds it.
8. For allegory, state the scope of the second level and its systematic
   correspondences.
9. For allusion, identify the prior text, work, figure, or event, the marker,
   and the recognizing audience; record indeterminacy where recognition fails.
10. Record which correspondences the figure projects and which it blocks, and
    name the conclusions that are prohibited; do not record the interpretive
    conclusion itself, which belongs to the interpretation owner.
11. Retain competing readings and cultural variation, and route the reading
    itself to the interpretation owner.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon candidate delta |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary | separates metaphor, metonymy, symbol, allegory, and allusion | joins them by mechanism type rather than by definition alone |
| Rhetorical handbook | catalogues named figures | keeps a bounded mechanism set instead of a figure catalogue |
| Cognitive linguistics | proposes systematic conceptual mappings | separates expression from claimed conceptual structure |
| Identification method | tests whether a word is used metaphorically | supplies a precondition without settling the reading |
| Semiotics | fixes the conventional sign class | excludes that sense of symbol by name |
| Literary theory | debates symbol, allegory, and intertextuality | preserves the disagreement instead of choosing a school |
| Formula symbol contract | declares notational tokens | keeps notation outside figurative signification |

## Failure signs

- a figure is recorded without a stated basic meaning to contrast against;
- metaphor and metonymy are used interchangeably;
- simile is treated as a weaker synonym of metaphor without noting the marking;
- the notational or sign-theoretic symbol is analyzed as a literary symbol;
- allegorical reading is recorded as a property of the work;
- an allusion is asserted without an identified prior text, work, figure, or event, or without a marker;
- motif recurrence is recorded as figurative signification;
- `analogy` is re-owned here instead of cross-referenced;
- a metaphor is used to license equivalence, causation, or shared structure;
- one theory of metaphor is presented as settled;
- the entry is used to decide which reading is correct.
