## Orientation

This candidate separates mathematical size and order type, positional lookup,
relationship multiplicity, and ordered measurement values. It does not merge
these into comparative quantity, evaluation score, or database schema design.

## Root factorization

Factor collection or domain, equivalence criterion, ordering relation, tie rule,
index base, sequence, relation ends, multiplicity constraint, scale, coding,
and admissible operations. These factors prevent mathematical and evaluation
claims from being conflated.

## Decisive distinctions

Cardinality is set size, not rank. Ordinality concerns order structure; rank is
a relative place under a particular comparison; an index is an address and can
be arbitrary. Relationship cardinality constrains relation instances, not set
size. Ordinal scale values order categories but do not warrant interval or
ratio arithmetic.

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Require an explicit domain and relation or scale. Route quantities and units to
quantity owners, scores and criteria to evaluation, data storage conventions to
information/data owners, and temporal sequence to temporal organization.

## Selection procedure

Ask whether the claim counts a set, orders elements, assigns a relative rank,
addresses a sequence, constrains a relation, or reports a category. Then record
the relevant equivalence, ordering, tie, coding, or schema convention.

## Reference Delta

Adds carefully separated mathematical and representational senses without
claiming ownership of all database or evaluation vocabulary.

## Failure signs

Failure includes averaging ordinal categories without a justified scale,
treating a database key as an ordinal, or reading `many-to-many` as a
measurement result.
