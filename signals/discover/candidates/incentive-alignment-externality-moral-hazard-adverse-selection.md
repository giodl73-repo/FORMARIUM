# Incentive, Alignment, Externality, Moral Hazard, and Adverse Selection

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft; not financial, legal, or policy
advice

## Orientation

This entry advances two conjoined contracts. They are stated separately because
each can fail on its own, and the entry should be read as making two claims, not
one.

**Contract A, the designed contingency relation.** An incentive is a consequence
that one party deliberately makes contingent on a named observable in order to
change another party's choice. Incentive compatibility is the accompanying
property claim: that under a stated mechanism and a stated equilibrium concept,
each agent's own payoff-maximizing behavior coincides with the designer's
intended behavior. That property does not hold by assertion; the mechanism, the
equilibrium concept, and the status of the demonstration are recorded, and the
property holds only under the assumptions of that demonstration. Conflating a
designed inducement with a demonstrated property is this entry's most common
failure. Contract A fails if cost and value, objective and indicator,
obligation, requirement statement, policy instrument, and delegation already
express every case without losing the conditioning observable, the timing, or
the choice intended to change.

**Contract B, the information-asymmetry and incidence conditions.** Alignment,
externality, moral hazard, and adverse selection name the conditions under which
pursued ends diverge, effects fall on non-parties, care or effort changes after
an agreement, and pool composition shifts before one. Contract B fails if
preference, outcome, causal attribution, risk, sampling, and compliance already
express every case without losing asymmetry timing, effect sign and non-party
incidence, ends-attribution basis, or proxy divergence.

The entry claims the relation and the conditions, and nothing else. The
magnitude belongs to cost, price, value, utility, and return. The target and its
measure belong to objective, indicator, and threshold. The rule or policy
instrument belongs to policy. A binding duty on a subject is an obligation and
belongs to governance; a stated evaluable requirement or specification belongs
to the requirement owner. The delegation structure belongs to organization. An
incentive requires nothing of anyone: it changes payoffs, which is precisely
what distinguishes it from both an obligation and a requirement statement.

Alignment here is ends congruence between a principal's ends and an agent's
pursued objective, assessed over a stated scope, horizon, and distribution of
situations. Whose ends they are is never assumed: every alignment row records an
attribution basis for the principal's ends and for the agent's pursued
objective, whether avowal, mandate, contract, artifact, or inference. Alignment
of artificial systems is one domain instance of that relation. This sense is
bounded away from the technical homonym: aligning units, scales, dimensions,
time bases, boundaries, or record keys is a measurement and quantity matter and
is not this entry's sense of the word.

Moral hazard and adverse selection are separated by timing and by what drives
them: post-agreement change in action or care, whether from unobservability or
shifted incidence, versus private type before the agreement. Two readings
of moral hazard are preserved rather than merged. On the observability reading,
the actor's effort or care cannot be observed by the counterparty. On the price
reading, the actor's behavior changes because the incidence of consequences
changed and the marginal price of the behavior fell, whether or not anything is
hidden; insurance that lowers the marginal cost of care produces this effect
even under full observation. Neither reading imputes moral fault. Moral hazard
is also a homonym of hazard as a source of potential harm.

An externality is an uncompensated effect falling on a party outside the
declared decision or transaction boundary. It is sign-neutral: an uncompensated
benefit conferred on a non-party is as much an externality as an uncompensated
harm, and every row records the sign. Its existence does not by itself establish
a wrong or warrant intervention; the Pareto-relevance test and the reciprocal
character of harm are both preserved.

Incentives are external and designed. What an agent wants, is settled on, or
pursues is internal and belongs to the motive, desire, intention, and rationale
candidate. The interaction between them, including crowding out of an existing
motive, is a factor recorded on the incentive row.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `incentive` | Which contingent consequence, offered by whom to whom, conditional on which observable, is intended to change which choice? | designed contingent inducement |
| `incentive-compatibility` | Under which mechanism and equilibrium concept is it demonstrated that each agent's own payoff-maximizing behavior coincides with the designer's intended behavior? | mechanism property whose holding must be demonstrated |
| `alignment` | Between which principal's ends, attributed on what basis, and which agent's pursued objective is divergence assessed, over what scope, horizon, and distribution of situations? | ends-congruence relation |
| `externality` | Which uncompensated effect of a decision, beneficial or harmful, falls on a party outside the decision or transaction boundary, and is it Pareto-relevant? | uncompensated non-party incidence |
| `moral-hazard` | Which action, effort, or care changes after an agreement because the actor no longer bears the full consequence, whether through unobservability or through a changed marginal price? | post-agreement behavior change under shifted incidence |
| `adverse-selection` | Which pre-agreement private information about type causes pool composition to shift under a common term or price? | pre-agreement hidden information |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Incentive vs. price | both are consequences with a magnitude | consequence made contingent on an observable to shift a choice vs. an exchange term for a good or service |
| Incentive vs. reward or compensation | both transfer value | designed contingency aimed at a specific choice vs. payment for work or outcome delivered |
| Incentive vs. obligation | both bear on conduct | changes payoffs and requires nothing vs. a binding duty on a subject from an authority source |
| Incentive vs. requirement statement | both can appear in a contract or program | payoff contingency vs. a stated evaluable condition subject to verification and validation |
| Incentive vs. policy instrument | both are deliberate interventions | the contingency itself vs. the rule or policy through which it may be enacted |
| Incentive vs. governance control | both are deliberate interventions | payoff contingency aimed at a choice vs. a measure that modifies risk against an objective |
| Incentive vs. delegation | both involve a principal and an agent | the contingency designed vs. the decision right transferred |
| Incentive vs. motive | both bear on why an agent acts | externally designed contingency vs. the agent's own end or concern |
| Incentive vs. incentive compatibility | both concern designed behavior change | a specific inducement offered vs. a property claimed of a mechanism and demonstrated under an equilibrium concept |
| Demonstrated vs. asserted incentive compatibility | both are stated as properties | a named mechanism, equilibrium concept, and demonstration vs. an unsupported claim |
| Alignment vs. compliance | both compare conduct to a standard | congruence of pursued ends vs. assessed conformity to applicable obligations |
| Alignment vs. agreement | both concern parties converging | congruence of ends vs. mutual assent forming a commitment |
| Alignment vs. indicator or target | both involve a measure | divergence of a rewarded proxy from the intended end vs. the measure and its threshold |
| Alignment (ends) vs. alignment (units) | both use the word alignment | ends congruence between a principal and an agent vs. reconciliation of units, scales, dimensions, or time bases |
| Attributed ends vs. assumed ends | both appear in an alignment claim | ends traced to an identified party by avowal, mandate, contract, artifact, or inference vs. ends supplied by the analyst |
| Externality vs. cost | both are sacrifices or effects | uncompensated effect on a non-party outside the declared boundary vs. a sacrifice under a declared accounting perspective |
| Positive vs. negative externality | both are uncompensated non-party effects | conferred benefit vs. imposed harm; the sign is recorded, not presumed |
| Externality vs. outcome | both are effects | uncompensated incidence on a non-party vs. a realized result relative to an objective |
| Externality vs. causal contribution | both link an act to an effect | boundary, sign, and compensation status vs. the causal relation itself |
| Pareto-relevant vs. inframarginal externality | both are real externalities | remaining mutual gain is available from adjustment vs. no such gain |
| Moral hazard vs. adverse selection | both are pre- or post-agreement conditions that shift behavior or pool composition | behavior change after the agreement, whether from unobservability or shifted incidence, vs. hidden type before it |
| Observability reading vs. price reading of moral hazard | both are post-agreement behavior changes | effort or care cannot be observed vs. incidence shifted and the marginal price of the behavior fell, with or without hiddenness |
| Moral hazard vs. hazard | both use the word hazard | incentive-induced change in care or effort vs. a source of potential harm |
| Moral hazard vs. fault | both can precede a loss | rational response to changed exposure vs. blameworthy conduct |
| Adverse selection vs. sampling selection | both distort a population | self-selection by informed parties under a common term or price vs. a defect in how a sample was drawn |
| Screening vs. signaling | both address hidden type | the uninformed party designs terms to separate types vs. the informed party incurs a cost to reveal type |
| Agency cost vs. incentive | both arise from principal-agent structure | monitoring, bonding, and residual loss as an amount vs. the contingency designed to reduce them |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

- Every incentive row names the designer, the target agent, the observable the
  consequence is conditioned on, the timing, the magnitude basis, and the
  specific choice intended to change.
- The magnitude itself, the objective and its indicator, the rule or policy
  instrument, the obligation, the requirement statement, and the delegation
  structure are cited, not redefined.
- Incentive compatibility rows name the mechanism, the equilibrium concept, and
  whether the property has been demonstrated and under which assumptions;
  without those, the row is an incentive claim, not a property claim.
- Alignment rows name the principal, the agent, the ends compared, the basis on
  which each set of ends is attributed, the scope, the horizon, and the
  distribution of situations over which divergence is assessed.
- Alignment in this entry never means unit, scale, dimension, time-base,
  boundary, or key alignment; those remain with the quantity and comparison
  owners.
- Externality rows declare the decision or transaction boundary, the affected
  non-party, the effect sign, the compensation status, and whether the
  externality is Pareto-relevant.
- Moral hazard rows state what changed after the agreement and why: whether
  effort or care became unobservable, whether incidence and marginal price
  changed, or both. Fault is not implied by either reading.
- Adverse selection rows state what type information was private before the
  agreement and how pool composition shifted; defects in how a sample was drawn
  belong to the sampling owner.
- Agency cost, multitask distortion, screening, signaling, risk sharing,
  crowding out, internalization instruments, and specification gaming remain
  factors rather than senses.
- Cost, choice, objective and control, governance, requirement, organization,
  policy, hazard, risk, sampling, agreement, and quantity retain their existing
  owners.

This entry stops before catalogs of compensation schemes and pay-for-performance
programs, carbon pricing and environmental policy design, insurance product
design, contract-theory proofs and equilibrium derivations, behavioral nudge
catalogs, corporate governance codes, and artificial-intelligence safety
technique catalogs.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify the principal or designer, the agent or respondent, and the specific
   decision intended to change.
2. State whether the question falls under the designed contingency relation,
   giving incentive or incentive compatibility, or under the asymmetry and
   incidence conditions, giving alignment, externality, moral hazard, or adverse
   selection.
3. For an incentive, record the contingent consequence, the observable it is
   conditioned on, the timing, the delivery, and the magnitude basis owned
   elsewhere.
4. For an incentive-compatibility claim, name the mechanism, the equilibrium
   concept, the assumptions, and whether the property was demonstrated or merely
   asserted.
5. Record the information asymmetry, its direction, and whether it precedes or
   follows the agreement, and classify accordingly.
6. For post-agreement behavior change, record whether the driver is
   unobservability, a changed incidence and marginal price, or both, and state
   that no fault is implied.
7. Record the action space and effort dimensions, and check whether rewarding a
   measured dimension distorts unmeasured ones.
8. For alignment, name the principal's ends and the agent's pursued objective,
   record the attribution basis for each, and state the scope, horizon, and
   distribution of situations; do not use the word for unit, scale, or dimension
   reconciliation.
9. Separate the rewarded proxy from the end it stands for and record the
   divergence and its verification cost.
10. For an externality, declare the decision boundary, name the affected
    non-party, record the effect sign, state compensation status, and apply the
    Pareto-relevance test before concluding that intervention is warranted.
11. Record participation, self-selection, screening, and signaling separately
    from effort and care, and separately from sampling design.
12. Record the observed response, including gaming, crowding out of existing
    motives, and the revision made in consequence.
13. Preserve incentives that failed, alignment reached without incentives,
    positive externalities, externalities that warrant no action, and hazards
    that involve no fault.
14. Route magnitude, objective, indicator, rule, obligation, requirement
    statement, delegation, risk, sampling, and unit questions to their existing
    owners.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon candidate delta |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary | treats incentive as anything that motivates | restricts the sense to a designed contingency on a named observable |
| Economics of information | models hidden action and hidden type | keeps the timing split explicit and preserves both the observability and the marginal-price readings |
| Mechanism design | states incentive compatibility under an equilibrium concept | separates the demonstrated property from the offered inducement and records demonstration status |
| Welfare economics | analyzes divergence between private and social product | requires a declared boundary, an effect sign, and the Pareto-relevance test |
| Management and organization research | documents rewarded proxies diverging from intended ends | keeps indicator and threshold with their existing owner |
| Standard or risk framework | structures risk-managed treatment of system behavior | prevents a framework label from settling alignment semantics or supplying unattributed ends |

## Failure signs

- incentive is used for anything that influences behavior, including the agent's
  own ends;
- an incentive row omits the observable, the timing, or the choice intended to
  change;
- an incentive is recorded as an obligation, or a requirement statement is
  recorded as an incentive;
- a designed inducement is called incentive compatible with no mechanism,
  equilibrium concept, or demonstration recorded;
- alignment is asserted without naming whose ends, on what attribution basis,
  over what scope and horizon;
- ends alignment and unit, scale, or dimension alignment share one field;
- proxy divergence is reported as achievement of the end;
- every unpriced effect is labeled an externality without a declared boundary;
- only harms are recorded as externalities and conferred benefits are dropped;
- an externality is treated as automatically a wrong;
- moral hazard is used as an accusation of bad faith;
- moral hazard is recognized only where something is hidden, so changes driven
  by a shifted marginal price are missed;
- moral hazard and adverse selection share one field, or either is reduced to
  generic risk;
- moral hazard is confused with hazard as a source of harm;
- adverse selection is used for a defect in how a sample was drawn;
- agency cost magnitudes are redefined here instead of cited;
- crowding out of intrinsic motivation is ignored after an incentive is
  introduced;
- the entry is used as financial, legal, or policy advice.
