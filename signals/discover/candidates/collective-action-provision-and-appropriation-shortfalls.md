# Collective Action Provision and Appropriation Shortfalls

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft

## Orientation

This family describes two structural shortfalls in which individually rational
choices can leave a group worse off. A `provision-shortfall` concerns too little
contribution to a shared benefit; an `appropriation-shortfall` concerns too much
extraction from a shared stock. Neither conclusion follows from a resource label
alone, and neither is inevitable: the applicable payoff structure, access
conditions, observation, and governing arrangement must be stated.

Excludability cost and rivalry intensity are continuous factors. Public,
common-pool, club, and private labels are diagnostic examples, not senses.
Free-riding, group size, monitoring, sanctions, and governance mechanisms are
likewise factors or possible responses, not alternative senses.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `provision-shortfall` | Under which benefit, payoff, and access conditions does individually rational non-contribution leave a shared benefit under-provided relative to the stated collective criterion? | collective under-provision condition |
| `appropriation-shortfall` | Under which stock-flow, payoff, rivalry, and access conditions does individually rational extraction deplete a shared resource stock beyond the stated sustainable or collective criterion? | collective over-appropriation condition |

## Root factorization

```text
collective-action-shortfall-use
  := affected group, boundary, and collective criterion
   x shared benefit or resource system, resource units, and stock-flow basis
   x excludability cost and rivalry intensity
   x public, common-pool, club, or private label where useful as an example
   x contribution or extraction action, actor set, group size, and heterogeneity
   x individual and collective payoff structure and benefit-production function
   x access conditions and free-rider condition where present
   x observability, monitoring, communication, repetition, and time horizon
   x existing institution, governance mechanism, and sanctioning arrangement
   x provision gap or appropriation/depletion rate, evidence, uncertainty, and revision
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Provision vs. appropriation shortfall | individually rational actions can be collectively adverse | provision needs additional contribution to a benefit; appropriation needs restraint of extraction from a stock |
| Provision shortfall vs. incentive | both concern payoffs and participation | the shortfall is the pre-design structural condition; an incentive is a deliberately designed payoff contingency |
| Provision shortfall vs. externality | both can involve uncompensated effects | this is a within-group contribution structure and need not involve a non-party outside a transaction boundary |
| Appropriation shortfall vs. stock-flow balance | both use stock and flow | stock-flow balance records accounting or continuity; this sense records a strategic extraction condition relative to a collective criterion |
| Appropriation shortfall vs. access authorization | both concern who may use a resource | authorization is a decision or permission mechanism; this sense is the payoff-and-rivalry condition that can remain despite access rules |
| Common-pool/public/club/private labels vs. either sense | each can describe a resource context | labels classify examples; they do not establish either shortfall without the stated payoff, access, and collective criterion |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

- State the collective criterion rather than infer it from an individual actor's
  interest, and preserve uncertainty about the payoff or depletion model.
- Treat excludability cost, rivalry intensity, free-rider condition, group size,
  monitoring, sanctioning, and benefit-production function as factors.
- Route designed incentives and adverse-selection or moral-hazard analysis to
  [Incentive, Alignment, Externality, Moral Hazard, and Adverse Selection](incentive-alignment-externality-moral-hazard-adverse-selection.md).
- Route institutions, governance mechanisms, obligations, and enforcement to
  [Governance, Obligation, and Compliance](../../../tables/entries/governance-obligation-compliance.md)
  and the institution candidate; route access decisions to
  [Access, Permission, Authorization, and Entitlement](../../../tables/entries/access-permission-authorization-entitlement.md).
- Route stock and flow accounting to
  [Stock, Flow, Accumulation, Balance, and Conservation](../../../tables/entries/stock-flow-balance.md),
  and trust conditions to the trust candidate. This family does not prescribe
  privatization, public management, sanctions, or any safety, policy, or
  governance intervention.

## Selection procedure

1. Name the group, subject boundary, and collective provision or sustainability
   criterion.
2. Select `provision-shortfall` for insufficient contribution to a benefit, or
   `appropriation-shortfall` for excessive extraction from a stock.
3. Record the benefit-production or stock-flow model, access conditions,
   excludability cost, rivalry intensity, and payoff structure.
4. Record group size, observability, repeated interaction, monitoring,
   sanctioning, and existing governance only as conditions or responses.
5. State the evidence and uncertainty for the claimed gap; do not infer a
   shortfall merely from a public/common-pool/club/private label.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon candidate delta |
|---|---|---|
| Public-goods theory | identifies non-rival consumption and contribution problems | keeps benefit structure separate from a universal resource taxonomy |
| Commons scholarship | distinguishes provision from appropriation and stock from resource units | preserves the two questions without making one governance model mandatory |
| Collective-action theory | models contribution, group size, and selective incentives | treats its predictions as factor-dependent rather than universal |
| Club-good analysis | shows access and congestion can vary continuously | prevents four familiar labels from becoming senses |

## Failure signs

- treating provision and appropriation as the same problem;
- inferring a shortfall from a resource label without a payoff and collective
  criterion;
- treating free-rider condition, group size, or a monitoring mechanism as a
  sense;
- making excludability or rivalry binary and universal;
- presenting Hardin's open-access outcome as a prediction for governed common
  property;
- re-owning incentive design, institutions, access authorization, stock-flow
  accounting, trust, or governance mechanisms.

   https://doi.org/10.2307/1925895 — contribution: non-rival collective
   consumption; limitation: it does not supply a general theory of governance or
   appropriation.
2. Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990) — contribution: provision and
   appropriation as distinct collective-action problems; limitation: its
   institutional findings are not universal laws.
3. Ostrom, “A General Framework for Analyzing Sustainability of
   Social-Ecological Systems” (2009), https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1172133 —
   contribution: resource-system/resource-unit and contextual-factor framing;
   limitation: a domain framework does not define every collective shortfall.

   https://doi.org/10.2307/2552442 — contribution: congestion and exclusion as
   variable conditions; limitation: club analysis does not subsume commons
   appropriation.
