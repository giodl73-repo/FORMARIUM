# Deontic Obligation, Permission, Prohibition, Optionality, Waiver, and Exemption

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft

[Admission brief](../literature/deontic-operators-candidate-brief-2026-08-20.md)

## Orientation

This candidate describes source-relative modal positions in a formal system or
bounded normative account. It leaves domain governance, access authorization,
and enforcement with their current owners and does not turn deontic notation
into legal advice.

## Sense table

| Sense ID | Governing question | Core |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `deontic-obligation` | Under which source and interpretation is the subject required to do or maintain the stated content? | required content |
| `deontic-permission` | Under which source and interpretation is the stated content allowed? | allowed content |
| `deontic-prohibition` | Under which source and interpretation is the stated content disallowed? | forbidden content |
| `deontic-optionality` | Under which source are both doing and omitting the stated content permitted? | bilateral permission |
| `deontic-waiver` | Which holder relinquishes or declines enforcement of which claim or requirement under what source? | holder-relative relinquishment |
| `deontic-exemption` | Which source removes a subject or case from an otherwise applicable requirement? | applicability exception |

## Root factorization

Factor source, system, subject, content, scope, time, priority, exception,
holder, authority, interpretation, and evidence. Source relativity is a guard,
not a sense.

## Decisive distinctions

Permission is not mere absence of a known prohibition. Optionality requires both
alternatives permitted. A waiver concerns a holder and an enforceable position;
an exemption changes applicability for a subject or case. Prohibition is not
physical impossibility, and obligation is not a prediction of compliance.

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Require an explicit source or formal system. Route access grants and
authorization to access owners, organization-specific governance to governance
owners, and decisions/exceptions to policy owners. Do not decide validity,
jurisdiction, enforcement, or moral correctness.

## Selection procedure

Declare the source and interpretation; identify content and scope; test the
opposite modal claim; then distinguish holder waiver from source exemption.
Where sources conflict, retain priority and unresolved status rather than
silently normalize.

## Reference Delta

Adds a formal comparative vocabulary around existing governance records, not a
replacement rule engine.

## Failure signs

Reject universal readings, “not forbidden therefore permitted” in systems that
do not support it, and waiver claims without a holder or source.
