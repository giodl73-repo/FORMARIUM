# Pattern, Regularity, Motif, Template, and Pattern Formation

Status: noncanonical candidate entry draft; descriptive, not explanatory

## Orientation

A regularity is a relation that repeats across two or more individuated
instances of a subject, corpus, or space, over a stated extent. Cross-instance
plurality is criterial: a single figure that is invariant under a
transformation family is a shape question and belongs to the canonical
geometric entry, not to this family. A pattern description is the explicit rule,
grammar, or specification that generates or recognizes the instances. A motif
is the smallest recurring element that persists across occurrences and is
identified by an index or catalogue. A template is a reusable prototype
instantiated through declared variable slots over a fixed structure. A design
pattern is a named unit of context, recurring problem, solution, and
consequences. Pattern formation is the mechanism by which a pattern arises from
initially uniform or unstructured conditions.

This entry owns recurrence across instances. It does not own the shape of one
figure under a transformation family, the composition of one whole, the
statistical validity of an inferred regularity, the model that explains a
mechanism, perceptual grouping, or staged change of a subject.

The following are excluded by name and routed. `gestalt` is perceptual
organization and belongs to the perception candidate. `metamorphosis` is staged
change and belongs to the transformation root and the state and lifecycle
entry. Shape-equivalence and set-theoretic image-operator `morphology`, and
geometric `form`, belong to the canonical geometric entry. Hylomorphic `form`,
as the form of a bearer or the form of a general realized in a particular,
belongs to the type and token candidate. Linguistic `morphology` belongs to the
morpheme and word-formation candidate. The sewing or casting pattern and
Gestalt therapy are unrelated and are excluded outright.

Only two of these are closed false gaps: gestalt, owned by perceptual
organization, and metamorphosis, owned by the transformation root together with
the state and lifecycle entry. Hylomorphic form and geometric morphology are
routed to owners named above. Linguistic morphology, urban morphology, and
musical or aesthetic form were never closed by any owner: they were unowned.
Linguistic morphology now has its own candidate. Urban morphology and musical
or aesthetic form remain open domain-specific residue, recorded here as
unowned rather than falsely closed, and no universal `form` sense is proposed
to absorb them.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `regularity` | Which relation repeats across two or more individuated instances, over which extent? | cross-instance repetition |
| `pattern-description` | Which explicit rule, grammar, or specification generates or recognizes the instances? | generative or recognitional rule |
| `motif` | Which smallest recurring element persists across occurrences, and by which index is it identified? | minimal recurring unit |
| `template` | Which reusable prototype is instantiated with which variable slots and which fixed structure? | parameterized prototype |
| `design-pattern` | Which recurring problem in which context does this named solution resolve, and with what consequences? | named problem-solution unit |
| `pattern-formation` | By which mechanism does a pattern arise from initially uniform or unstructured conditions? | generative mechanism |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern vs. shape | both concern form | a repetition relation across instances vs. properties preserved under a transformation family for one figure |
| Pattern vs. structure | both describe organization | recurrence across instances vs. the composition and dependency of one whole |
| Pattern vs. correlation | both are statistical | any describable regularity vs. a specific measured dependence between variables |
| Regularity vs. symmetry of one figure | both are non-random structure | a relation repeating across two or more individuated instances vs. invariance of one figure under a transformation family, owned by the geometric entry |
| Regularity vs. law | both generalize | an observed repetition, possibly accidental vs. a supported general claim with explanatory standing |
| Regularity vs. pattern description | both concern the same instances | what was observed vs. the rule that generates or recognizes it |
| Motif vs. theme | both recur in works | a concrete smallest recurring element vs. an abstract subject running through the work |
| Motif vs. tale type | both appear in folkloric indices | the smallest persistent element vs. a whole traditional narrative type |
| Motif vs. symbol | both persist meaningfully | recurrence is the criterion vs. surplus signification is the criterion |
| Template vs. pattern | both are reusable | a fillable prototype with declared slots vs. a recurring relation that need not be instantiable |
| Design pattern vs. algorithm | both are reusable solutions | a named context, problem, solution, and consequences unit requiring adaptation vs. a determinate procedure |
| Design pattern vs. template | both are named reusable units | a solution with stated consequences and tradeoffs vs. a prototype with slots |
| Pattern vs. pattern formation | both concern patterns | the described regularity vs. the mechanism producing it from uniform conditions |
| Pattern formation vs. model | both explain | the claimed generative mechanism vs. the represented and validated model of it |
| Pattern vs. artifact | both are perceived regularities | a regularity surviving declared validity checks vs. one attributable to sampling, multiplicity, or observer bias |
| Pattern vs. gestalt | both are wholes | a repetition relation in the subject vs. a perceptual whole organized for a perceiver |
| Pattern vs. metamorphosis | both are described across multiple points or occurrences | a relation repeating across two or more individuated instances vs. staged reorganization of one subject across its own developmental points |
| Template vs. instantiation of a general | both produce a case | a reusable prototype filled through declared slots vs. a particular being a case of a general, owned by the type and token candidate |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

- The subject, corpus, or space is identified, with observation extent,
  resolution, and boundary, before a pattern is claimed.
- At least two individuated instances are required. A relation observed on one
  figure or one occurrence is not a regularity here; single-figure invariance
  routes to the canonical geometric entry.
- The repeated unit has an individuation criterion, and the repetition relation
  is named.
- The instance set is recorded together with exceptions and near-misses.
- Recognition method, threshold, and detector or observer are recorded; a
  pattern found by a detector is attributed to that detector.
- The pattern-versus-artifact guard is mandatory and routes statistical validity to
  the sampling and estimation owners; it does not create a second statistics
  owner.
- A generative mechanism is optional, separately stated, and routed to the
  model owner; description never implies mechanism.
- Naming, catalogues, indices, and pattern languages are membership facts, not
  validity claims.
- Shape and transformation equivalence, system composition and dependency,
  perceptual organization, and staged change retain their existing or candidate
  owners.
- The general-to-particular relation retains the type and token candidate as
  its owner. This family owns the reusable prototype and its declared slots; it
  does not own instantiation of a general by a particular.
- Linguistic morphology retains the morpheme and word-formation candidate as
  its owner; urban morphology and musical or aesthetic form remain unowned
  residue rather than gaps closed here.

This entry stops before specific pattern catalogues, named design patterns,
named motif or tale-type index numbers, pattern-recognition algorithms, image
processing operators, discipline-specific morphologies, and the aesthetic value
of any pattern.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify the subject, corpus, population, medium, or space, with extent,
   resolution, sampling, and boundary.
2. State whether the question concerns regularity, pattern description, motif,
   template, design pattern, pattern formation, or the artifact check.
3. Exclude gestalt, metamorphosis, geometric and mathematical morphology,
   hylomorphic form, linguistic morphology, and the craft and therapy senses by
   name, and route each to its owner.
4. Confirm that at least two individuated instances are available; if only one
   figure or occurrence is in scope, route the question to the geometric entry.
5. Name the repeated unit and its individuation criterion.
6. Name the repetition relation, such as translation, reflection, rotation,
   scaling, sequence, or co-occurrence.
7. Record the instance set, admissible variation, exceptions, and near-misses.
8. Record the recognition method, threshold, and detector or observer.
9. For a template, list variable slots and fixed structure; for a design
   pattern, list context, problem, solution, consequences, and tradeoffs; for a
   motif, name the index or catalogue and its editorial limits.
10. State a generative mechanism only as a separate claim, and route it to the
    model owner.
11. Run the artifact check for sampling, multiplicity, and confirmation before
    reporting the pattern as a property of the subject.
12. Route shape equivalence, composition, statistical estimation, perceptual
    organization, staged change, and the general-to-particular relation to
    their existing owners.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon candidate delta |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary | separates pattern, motif, template, and form | joins them by recurrence and separates the excluded senses by name |
| Architecture and software pattern catalogue | supplies named problem-solution units | keeps catalogues in mapping views and retains consequences and tradeoffs |
| Folkloristics index | catalogues motifs and tale types | records index membership and its editorial and cultural limits |
| Mathematical biology | explains pattern formation from uniform conditions | keeps mechanism separate from description |
| Statistical learning | detects regularities and warns about generalization | routes validity to the sampling and estimation owners |
| Geometry | fixes shape under a transformation family | shows what one-figure equivalence cannot express |
| Perceptual psychology | explains grouping and figure-ground organization | routes gestalt out instead of re-owning it |
| Metaphysics of form | distinguishes accidental from substantial change | routes hylomorphic form to the type and token candidate instead of owning it |
| Linguistic morphology | analyzes word-internal structure | routes to its own candidate rather than being absorbed as recurrence |

## Failure signs

- a regularity is claimed from a single figure or a single occurrence;
- a pattern is claimed without an instance set or a named repetition relation;
- shape equivalence or symmetry for one figure is reported as a pattern;
- system composition is reported as a pattern;
- a description is treated as a mechanism, or a mechanism as evidence that this
  instance was so produced;
- the artifact check is skipped, or a detector's output is reported as a
  property of the subject;
- multiplicity, sampling, or confirmation bias is unrecorded;
- motif, template, and design pattern are used interchangeably;
- an index number is cited as if it settled a cultural claim;
- naming a design pattern is treated as validating its application;
- gestalt or metamorphosis is re-owned here;
- hylomorphic form or geometric morphology is analyzed here instead of routed;
- linguistic or urban morphology is analyzed as recurrence;
- an unowned residue such as urban morphology or musical form is reported as a
  closed false gap;
- a template's declared slots are used to claim the general-to-particular
  relation owned by the type and token candidate.
