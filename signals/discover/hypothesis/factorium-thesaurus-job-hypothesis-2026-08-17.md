# Factorium Thesaurus-Job Hypothesis

## Hypothesis

**Claim:** On a frozen internal portfolio, `sim-47` can perform a bounded
thesaurus job by routing familiar related language to the right existing Table
families and preserving ambiguity while its authored destinations expose the
distinctions a conventional synonym list cannot supply.

**Falsification condition:** The claim is wrong if any frozen class misses its
threshold, if an alias changes literal ranking, or if an ambiguous term is
made artificially singular.

**Confidence:** 65/100.

**Hypothesis class:** Technical. This predicts deterministic artifact behavior,
not what people will prefer or do.

## Prior

**Team belief before:** Factorium should feel more useful than a plain thesaurus
when the reader needs to distinguish neighboring concepts, but its current
search may still privilege literal occurrence over conceptual ownership.

**Origin:** Product thesis, authored Table structure, prior synthetic lookup
campaigns, LXC-01 lexical screening, and the five LXA-01 aliases.

| Evidence type | Threshold to shift belief |
|---|---|
| Exact alias mechanics | All 5 aliases expose their exact frozen owner sets without ranking change |
| Near-neighbor lookup | At least 4 of 5 queries surface every intended family within five unique families |
| Broader/narrower lookup | At least 4 of 5 queries surface every intended family within five unique families |
| Ambiguity preservation | At least 3 of 5 words surface two or more intended families within five unique families |
| Destination structure | Every passing non-alias task reaches an intended canonical page with authored contrast or cross-reference structure |

**Confirmation-bias check:** We may want to dismiss literal-search misses as
bad queries because the Tables themselves are strong. They remain failures of
this tested thesaurus route.

## Falsification tests

### Test 1 — lexical route portfolio

- Method: deterministic `sim-47` search and exact alias execution.
- Measure: intended canonical families among the first five unique families.
- Falsification threshold: any frozen category misses its threshold.
- Timeframe: immediate repository run.

### Test 2 — ambiguity and ranking custody

- Method: compare complete `sim-47` literal rankings with the alias-free
  `sim-46` index and inspect ambiguous owner sets.
- Measure: exact ranking equality and plural intended-family exposure.
- Falsification threshold: any ranking changes or fewer than three ambiguity
  tasks expose at least two intended families.
- Timeframe: same run.

### Test 3 — destination value structure

- Method: inspect canonical source pages reached by passing tasks.
- Measure: authored `Contrast table` or `Cross-references` section.
- Falsification threshold: any passing non-alias task lacks both structures at
  its intended destination.
- Timeframe: same run.

If all tests survive, the result establishes internally rehearsed mechanics
only. Human recognition, usefulness, preference, comprehension, and return use
remain unknown.

## Frozen design findings

1. A thesaurus job begins with language rather than canonical IDs.
2. Exact synonyms are only one thesaurus behavior.
3. Near-neighbor distinction is a separate job.
4. Broader/narrower grouping is a separate job.
5. Ambiguous ordinary words need plural routes.
6. Canonical-family identity is the scoring unit.
7. Specialized views must not inflate family coverage.
8. Five unique families is the frozen visibility window.
9. Alias panels and literal rankings are measured separately.
10. Complete ranking equality protects the control.
11. Multi-owner field aliases cannot be scored as singular.
12. Authored contrast structure is product evidence, not reader evidence.
13. A miss does not automatically imply missing content.
14. A pass does not justify broad synonym expansion.
15. Null and partial category outcomes remain admissible.
