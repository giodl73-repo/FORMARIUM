# Formography Foundation Comparator Brief

The primary competitor is not another new field name. It is the ability to
describe the work adequately through existing methods.

| Comparator | Reuse | Adapt | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| STS formography | relational and multiple forms | explicit cognate contrast | claiming the word is new |
| concept maps | focus questions and labeled links | nonhierarchical alternative views | treating one diagram as the complete record |
| faceted classification | analytico-synthetic decomposition | open form-specific factors | fixed universal PMEST factors |
| Topic Maps | topics, associations, occurrences, scope | source and boundary representation | presenting encoding syntax as method authority |
| knowledge graphs | typed graph implementation | provenance and qualified edges | graph proximity as semantic proof |
| ontology engineering | explicit classes, properties, constraints, versions | local formal validation where earned | universal ontology as a foundation |
| Levine's formalism | interacting forms and affordances | cross-domain case analysis | fixed form types or purely hermeneutic validation |
| lexicography and thesauri | sense custody and semantic relations | reference-oriented reader routes | reducing forms to words |

## Whitespace hypothesis

The candidate whitespace is not "mapping concepts." It is a repeatable method
for producing inspectable, source-custodied maps in which:

- factor sets remain alternative and view-relative;
- relation direction, scope, qualifiers, and authority are explicit;
- context and boundaries are part of the record;
- projections declare what they omit;
- no one map is promoted as the universal ontology.

This whitespace must be demonstrated through comparative artifacts.
